How does the ATP ranking actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The ATP ranking is a 52-week rolling points tally of a player's best 19 tournaments — 4 Slams plus 8 Masters 1000 mandatories plus 7 best other — with 2000 points for a Slam, 1000 for a Masters, 500 for a 500, 250 for a 250, and no points for losing first round — the ranking is the total, not the average.

Explained properly

The 52-week roll explains movement. Points stay for 52 weeks then drop — a player who won the Australian Open for 2000 in January keeps them until next January, then defends them — so the ranking on Monday is last year's this week replaced, which is why Djokovic can be 1st without playing if his best 19 still totals more than Alcaraz's 19, and why a 3-month injury drops a player 30 places as 3 Masters worth 1000 each fall off with no replacement.

The 19 explain the ceiling. Four Slams (2000 each) plus eight Masters 1000 (1000 each) are mandatory if the player qualifies, so 12 of the 19 are fixed — the other 7 are best of the player's 500s, 250s, Challengers or United Cup — so a top 10 must play 12 plus about 7 more to keep 19, while a player ranked 80 plays 25 to find his best 7, which is why the top play 19 and the grinder plays 30 but the total is still best 19, not all.

Points per round show the ladder. Slam winner 2000, final 1200, semi 720, quarter 360, round of 16 180, round of 32 90, round of 64 45, round of 128 10 — so a quarter at a Slam is worth more than a 250 title (250), which is why a Slam quarter specialist can be top 20 without winning a title, and why a 250 title as a wild card is often the 19th that stays, not the top, for a top 10.

Why Masters are mandatory is the calendar. The 8 Masters — Indian Wells, Miami, Monte Carlo, Madrid, Rome, Canada, Cincinnati, Shanghai, Paris — are 96 or 56 draws, so 0 points for missing without medical exemption counts as a 0 in the 19, which is why a top player who skips Miami for rest takes a 0 that drags the total, not just a missed chance — the 0 is the evergreen myth that ranking is best 19, not 19 played, so a missed mandatory is a 0, not a free skip.

The live ranking vs the official explains Monday. The official ranking updates Monday after the Slam or Masters, but the live ranking during the tournament shows the points with last year's drop already removed — a player can be live 5th on Thursday and official 8th on Monday if the 2000 he defended last year was not re-earned this year, which is why the live moves during the event more than the official after.

Picture this: Monday after the US Open: Sinner won 2000 for the US Open, kept his 2000 from last year's Australian that did not yet drop, dropped 360 from last year's Rome quarter that is now 52 weeks old, added 180 for this year's Rome round of 16 as his 19th — total 9,200, still 1st over Alcaraz on 8,500 who added a 1000 Miami win but lost a 2000 from last year's Wimbledon final that dropped that Monday — 19 best, 52-week roll, Monday update.

Myths vs reality

“Ranking is all tournaments.”
Reality: Best 19 — 4 Slams plus 8 Masters mandatories plus 7 best other — so 12 are fixed and 7 are best, not all 30 played.
“Missing a Masters is free.”
Reality: Masters are mandatory — a missed mandatory without medical is a 0 in the 19, not a skip, so it drags the total like a first-round loss.
“Ranking is average.”
Reality: Total of best 19 over 52 weeks — the 52-week roll means points drop after 52 weeks, so a 3-month injury drops 30 places as 3 Masters fall off.

FAQ

How many points for a Slam?

2000 win, 1200 final, 720 semi, 360 quarter, 180 round of 16 — a Slam quarter beats a 250 title's 250.

How many tournaments count?

Best 19 — 4 Slams plus 8 Masters 1000 plus 7 best other over 52 weeks, dropping after 52 weeks.

What if a player skips a Masters?

0 points in the 19 if not medical — a mandatory 0, not a free skip, so the total drops.
