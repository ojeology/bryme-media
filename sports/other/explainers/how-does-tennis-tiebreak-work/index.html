How does a tennis tiebreak work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: A tiebreak is first to 7 by 2 at 6-6, with serve rotation 1-2-2-2, and the Slam final-set tiebreak since 2022 is first to 10 by 2 at 6-6 — the tiebreak replaces the endless deuce game at 6-6 to decide the set in about 20 minutes, not 60.

Explained properly

The 7-point explains the set. At 6-6, the tiebreak is first to 7 by 2 — the server serves 1 point, then the returner serves 2, 2, 2 until 7 — so the serve pattern is 1-2-2-2, change ends after 6 points, which is why the 6-6 game can end 7-6(3) where the winner took 7 points and loser 3, and why the score is 7-6(7-3), not 7-3 — the tiebreak is the 13th game of the set, not the set's second half.

The 10-point is the Slam final difference. Since 2022 every Slam plays final set at 6-6 as first to 10 by 2, not endless — Australian, French, Wimbledon and US Open all 10-point final-set super tiebreak, so the 6-6 final is 10-8, not 16-14 as Isner-Mahut 70-68 was in 2010's endless fifth before the law — the 10 by 2 saves 30 minutes and the 70-68 will never return, which is why the final-set 6-6 is now 10-point, not advantage.

Why 6-6 matters is the break. Before 6-6 the set is 6 games by 2 — 6-4, 6-3 — with no tiebreak until 6-6, so a 5-4 lead still needs a break to 6-4 or tiebreak, which is why a 6-5 service game is break point not set point, and why the tiebreak's first-to-7 is about 20 minutes, not the 45-minute 8-6 that 7-6 replaces — the tiebreak's 7 vs 10 decides 10 sets per Slam more than the 6-6 would have.

The myth is tiebreak is first to 7 always. The match tiebreak in doubles is first to 10 by 2 instead of a third set, so the doubles final can be 6-4, 6-7(3), 10-7 where the 10-7 is the match, not the set — the 10-point match vs 7-point set is the doubles bottleneck where the 3rd set is the 10-point, not a 6-4, which is why the doubles ranking includes the 10-point result as a half-set.

The Hawk-Eye myth is the let. The tiebreak has no lets on serve now since 2018 in the tiebreak, so the serve that clips and is in is played, which is why the tiebreak's 7 and 10 have more aces per point than the 6-6 game — the tiebreak's 1-2-2-2 rotation makes the 6th point the change, so the 3-3 end change is tactical, not random.

Picture this: Wimbledon final 2023: Alcaraz vs Djokovic final set 6-6 at 4:45, tiebreak first to 7 — Alcaraz wins 7-6(7-3) at 6:05, not 14-12, with serve 1-2-2-2 rotation — Slam final-set 6-6 now 10 by 2 since 2022, so US Open semi 6-6 is 10-7 not 9-7 — the 7 by 2 normal set and 10 by 2 final set that replaced Isner-Mahut 70-68 11-hour fifth in 2010.

Myths vs reality

“Tiebreak is always 7 points.”
Reality: 7 by 2 at 6-6 for sets 1-4, 10 by 2 at 6-6 final set since 2022 — the match tiebreak in doubles is 10 by 2 for the deciding set.
“Tiebreak serve is alternate.”
Reality: 1 point then 2-2-2 — change ends after 6, so 3-3 is the tactical switch, not every point.
“Tiebreak decides the match.”
Reality: Decides the set 7-6(7-3) — final set tiebreak 10-8 is the 7-6(10-8) that 2010's endless 70-68 never will be again.

FAQ

How many points in a tiebreak?

First to 7 by 2 at 6-6 — serve 1 then 2-2-2 — final-set tiebreak since 2022 is first to 10 by 2.

What is 7-6(7-3)?

7-6 set, 7-3 tiebreak — winner took 7 points, loser 3, so 7-6(7-3) is 7-3 in the breaker, 7-6 in the set.

Doubles tiebreak same?

No, doubles uses 10-point match tiebreak instead of third set — 6-4, 6-7(3), 10-7 is the match, not a set.
