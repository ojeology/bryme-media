What is the powerplay in cricket?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The powerplay is the fielding restriction of overs 1-10 with 2 fielders outside the 30-yard circle, overs 11-40 with 4 outside, overs 41-50 with 5 outside — the 10-30-10 split in ODI that decides 320 par, not the batting team's choice, with T20's 6 overs powerplay 2 outside then 14 with 5.

Explained properly

The ODI powerplay is 10-30-10. Overs 1-10 2 fielders outside circle, 11-40 4 outside, 41-50 5 outside, plus 2 fielders in catching positions in 1-10 — the 10 with 2 outside forces the openers to hit over the top for 4s inside, which is why the powerplay 1-10 goes at 60 per 10 vs middle 40-45 per 10, but middle's 30 with 4 outside is the build, not slog — the 10-30-10 decides the 50's shape: powerplay 60, middle 135, death 125 =320 par.

The T20 powerplay is 6 overs with 2 outside, 14 with 5 outside. The T20 20 overs split 6 with 2 outside then 14 with 5 — the 6 is 50-55 vs 170 par, so the powerplay is 30% of innings, not 20% as ODI's 10 is 20% — the 6 with 2 makes the T20 opeler's 30 off 18 more valuable than ODI's 30 off 30, which is why the IPL's 6-over 55 sets the 170, not the ODI's 10-over 60, and why the 6's 2-outside is the 10's twin with 20% less time.

The batting choice powerplay is old ODI before 2015 — the batting powerplay 5 overs with 3 outside was chosen by batting side overs 11-40, removed 2015 to fix the 10-30-10 mandatory, which is why the 2023 World Cup's powerplay's 10-30-10 is not chosen — the 2011 World Cup had batting and bowling powerplays, the 2015 law replaced with 10-30-10 default.

The field circle matters. The 30-yard circle is 30 yards from stumps, within which 4 fielders must be in powerplay 1, 2 catchers in that 4 — the 4 inside the circle is the bottleneck that 2 outside leaves 7 inside including keeper and bowler, so the 2-outside powerplay is 7 inside, not 9 as 5 outside later, which is why the powerplay's 2 allows the loopy 4 over mid-off, not the flat 6 over extra cover vs 5 outside.

The evergreen myth is powerplay is batting choice. Since 2015 it is fixed 10-30-10 — the captain not the batter decides no choice, which is why the powerplay's 10-30-10 shape is 60 + 135 +125 =320, not the batter's 5-over choice, and why T20's 6 plus 5-outside 14 is also fixed, not optional, like football's 15-min halftime fixed, not the captain's.

Picture this: World Cup 2023 powerplay: Rohit Sharma 40 off 24 in first 10 with 2 outside vs Australia, middle 11-40 with 4 outside 135, death 41-50 with 5 outside 125 — 10-30-10 =320 par, T20's 6 with 2 then 14 with 5 =50 in 6 — the 10 with 2 to 30 with 4 to 10 with 5 that 2015 made mandatory, not batting choice, 30-yard circle 7 inside not 9.

Myths vs reality

“Powerplay is batter's choice.”
Reality: Fixed 10-30-10 ODI — 1-10 2 outside, 11-40 4 outside, 41-50 5 outside — not chosen since 2015, and T20's 6 plus 14 is also fixed.
“Powerplay is 20% of innings.”
Reality: 10 of 50 =20% ODI, but 6 of 20 =30% T20 — so T20 powerplay 55 in 6 sets 170, ODI's 60 in 10 plus middle 135 death 125 sets 320.
“Field is open in powerplay.”
Reality: Powerplay 1-10 only 2 outside means 7 inside — 4 in the 30-yard circle with 2 catchers — not the 5-outside's 6 inside later.

FAQ

What is powerplay in ODI?

1-10 2 fielders outside 30-yard circle, 11-40 4 outside, 41-50 5 outside — 10-30-10, not batter choice since 2015.

What is T20 powerplay?

1-6 2 outside, 7-20 5 outside — 6 overs of 20, 30% of innings, not 20% as ODI's 10.

Why does powerplay decide par?

Powerplay 60 per 10 plus middle 135 plus death 125 =320 par — the shape, not just the total, sets the 50-over chase.
