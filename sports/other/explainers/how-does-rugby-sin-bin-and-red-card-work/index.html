How do sin bins and red cards work in rugby?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Rugby cards are yellow 10 minutes (2 in sevens) with 14 men, red is 14 men rest of game but since 2024 the 20-minute red lets the team replace after 20, so the game returns to 15 vs 15 but the sent-off player is gone — the orange 20-minute vs permanent decides, not just yellow vs red as in football's red still 10 men.

Explained properly

The yellow is 10 minutes in 15s (2 in sevens) with 14 men — tackled for cynical offside, high tackle at shoulder with no head contact mitigation, repeated team offside 3 penalties in 10 — the sin bin's 10 is 12.5% of 80, sevens 2 is 14% of 14 so proportional — the player returns after 10, so the 14 vs 15 is temporary, which is why the penalty try plus yellow for collapsing the maul is 5 points plus 10 minutes, not just 5, and why the 2023 law's bunker review for head contact tests yellow or red before the 10 or permanent, not after as football's VAR red straight.

The 20-minute red replaces the tackled red permanently. Since 2024 the orange 20-minute red: a red card for head contact with mitigation can be 20-minute sanction — the team is 14 for 20 then 15 again with replacement, but the player not returning — a deliberate foul or dangerous with no mitigation is full permanent red with 14 rest of game — the 20 vs full is bunker TMOs decision with 8-minute window, which is why France vs Wales 2024 had a 20-minute red not permanent, the player off but team back to 15 after 20.

The high tackle decision tree explains yellow vs red. Tackle height above shoulder plus head contact plus force plus mitigation (dipper, no time, no control) — high plus head with no mitigation is red or 20-minute, high plus shoulder with mitigation is yellow 10 — the tree's 3: yellow, 20-minute, permanent, which is why the 20-minute exists for mitigation not mitigation's yellow vs red binary — the head's risk vs shoulder's level plus dip's mitigation's 2 steps decide, not the foot as football's DOGSO.

The evergreen myth is red always 14 vs 15 to end. The 20-minute red returns to 15 vs 15 after 20, so the game is numbers-equal after 20 unless permanent — the 20's replacement is a non-offending bench player, not the sent-off, so the team's 23 becomes 22 plus 15, which is why the 2024 England vs Ireland's 20-minute red at 62 minutes left 14 until 82, then 15 for the last 10, not 14 for last 20, like 6 Nations' 1 yellow vs permanent's full.

The penalty try plus card explains double. A maul collapse to stop try is penalty try 7 points plus yellow 10 for the tackler — the 7 plus 10 double where football's DOGSO is red alone with no penalty goal's 7 — the rugby 7 plus 10 is the double where football's red is 1 zero plus 10 vs 11 for rest, which is why the maul's penalty try plus yellow is 7 plus 10, not just 7 as football's no-card penalty would be.

Picture this: 2024 Six Nations: France flanker tackles high with head but dipper mitigation — bunker review 8 minutes, decision 20-minute red not permanent — 14 until 62+20=82 then 15 again, player gone, team back to 15 — yellow 10 with 14 men 12.5% of 80 vs sevens 2 of 14 — the tree yellow vs 20-min vs permanent with head contact plus mitigation decides, not football's DOGSO red's 10 vs 11 permanent alone.

Myths vs reality

“Red is 14 vs 15 to end.”
Reality: 20-minute red since 2024 — 14 for 20 then 15 again with replacement, player gone — permanent only for no mitigation.
“Yellow is just warning.”
Reality: 10 minutes with 14 men 12.5% of 80 — penalty try 7 plus yellow 10 for maul collapse, not just 7.
“High tackle is always red.”
Reality: Yellow, 20-min or permanent via bunker by head vs shoulder plus force plus dipper mitigation — tree decides, not binary.

FAQ

What is 20-minute red?

Since 2024 orange — 14 for 20 then 15 again with replacement, player gone — mitigation head contact gets 20-min, not permanent.

How long is yellow?

10 minutes 15s (2 minutes sevens) — 12.5% of 80 — team 14 vs 15, then player returns.

When is penalty try plus card?

Maul collapse to stop try — 7 points plus yellow 10 for the tackler — double where football is red alone.
