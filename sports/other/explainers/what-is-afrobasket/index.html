What is AfroBasket?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: AfroBasket is FIBA Africa's 16-team national-team championship every 4 years (next 2025) — 16 nations play 4 groups of 4 over 10 days, top 8 to the knockout, winner qualifies for the FIBA World Cup and the Olympics pre-qualifier, with 0.01 on the clock deciding, not a table.

Explained properly

AfroBasket is 16 of 54 — 16 qualify via 5 zones: Zone 1 North, Zone 2 West, Zone 3 West-Central, Zone 4 Central, Zone 5 East and Zone 6 South — Nigeria's D'Tigers and D'Tigress qualify via Zone 3, Tunisia via Zone 1, Angola via Zone 6, so the 16 are zonal champions plus wild cards, not the 16 best by ranking, which is why the qualifier in Monastir or Dakar can be harder than the group in Kigali.

The format is 4 groups of 4 over 10 days — groups in one city, then quarters, semis and final single elimination — so the group is 3 games in 4 days, then 3 knockouts in 5 days, which is why depth matters more than the NBA's 82: the D'Tigers' 12-man roster plays 6 games in 10 days, not 82 in 170, and the quarter is single game, not best-of-7, so Angola vs Nigeria in the quarter is one half to decide the World Cup ticket.

The ticket explains the evergreen. The AfroBasket winner plus top 5 qualify for the FIBA World Cup qualifiers' second window, and since 2023 the AfroBasket also feeds the Olympic pre-qualifier. Tunisia's 2021 win over Ivory Coast 78-75 in Kigali put them into the 2023 World Cup, which is why the AfroBasket is not just the continental title but the gate to the World Cup, like AFCON's group to knockout but with 16 not 24.

The women's twin is Afrobasket Women — 12 teams, same format, 2 groups of 6 then knockout, every 2 years, not 4 — D'Tigress have won 4 of the last 5, which is why the women's AfroBasket is more dominated than the men's where Tunisia, Angola and Senegal share the last 6.

The venue myth is the rotation. AfroBasket 2021 was Kigali, 2017 was Tunis/Dakar, 2025 is Angola — the host qualifies automatically, so Angola 2025 starts at 1-0 while the other 15 come from zones, which is why the host's 10 days at home is the edge that Tunisia used in 2015 and 2017 to win.

Picture this: Kigali 2021: Tunisia beat Ivory Coast 78-75 in the final after topping Group B 3-0 — 16 teams 4x4, 10 days, 3+3 knockout, winner to the FIBA World Cup 2023 — D'Tigers out in the quarter to Uganda on a last-second 3, so the 2021 AfroBasket quarter was the World Cup gate, not just the continental medal, with the 2025 edition in Angola as host auto-qualifier.

Myths vs reality

“AfroBasket is clubs.”
Reality: 16 national teams via 5 FIBA Africa zones — Nigeria's D'Tigers via Zone 3 qualifiers, not Rivers Hoopers as a club — winner to the FIBA World Cup gate.
“The winner is world champion.”
Reality: Continental champion plus gate to the World Cup — winner plus top 5 enter the FIBA World Cup qualifiers, not the World Cup itself.
“It is every year.”
Reality: Men every 4 years now (next 2025 Angola), women every 2 years — 12 women's teams, not 16.

FAQ

How many teams at AfroBasket?

16 nations via 5 zones, 4 groups of 4 over 10 days, 8 to single knockout — winner plus top 5 to the FIBA World Cup qualifiers.

When is AfroBasket?

Men every 4 years — next 2025 in Angola as host — women every 2 years with 12 teams.

Is AfroBasket the World Cup?

No, the continental gate — like AFCON's 16 of 54, not the World Cup's 32 of 211.
