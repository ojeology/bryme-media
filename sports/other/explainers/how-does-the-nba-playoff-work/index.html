How does the NBA playoff actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The NBA playoffs are 16 teams — 8 per East and West — seeded 1-8 after the Play-In for 7-10, then four best-of-7 rounds: first round, semis, conference finals and the Finals over about 2 months, with home court for the higher seed in a 2-2-1-1-1 format, and the Finals winner gets the Larry O'Brien trophy, not a single game like the NFL.

Explained properly

The NBA playoffs are 16 of 30 — East 8 and West 8 — after the Play-In where 7 vs 8 for 7th and 9 vs 10 then loser vs winner of 7/8 for 8th, so 10 of 15 per conference have a shot, 6 per conference are eliminated after 82 games. The 82-game regular season decides seeding, not elimination — the 10th can make the playoffs over the 9th with one Play-In win, which is why the Play-In's single game is football's relegation playoff but for the last playoff spot.

The format is 2-2-1-1-1. The higher seed hosts games 1, 2, 5 and 7, the lower hosts 3, 4 and 6 — so the 1 seed gets 4 home, the 8 gets 3, and a sweep is 4-0 in 10 days, a 7-game series is 7 over 15 days, which is why a first-round 1 vs 8 that goes 7 games gives the 1 seed 4 home crowds and the 8 three, but the 8 can still win Game 7 on the road, as Miami as 8 beat Milwaukee as 1 in 2023.

Why best-of-7 matters is variance. A single-game upset like March Madness is 1 in 10, but a 7-game series needs 4 wins, so the better team wins about 80% of 7-game series vs 65% of single games — the NBA playoff is the least random of the US playoffs for that reason, which is why 8 seeds beat 1 seeds only 6 times in NBA history but 16 seeds beat 1s twice in NCAA's single game, and why the Finals is almost always 1-4 vs 1-4, not 8 vs 8.

The Finals is best-of-7 in the same format — 2-2-1-1-1 with the higher regular-season record hosting, not the conference champion alone — so a 60-win West 1 can host the Finals over a 50-win East 1 even if the East 1 swept, which is why the 2023 Finals was Miami as 8 at Denver as 1 with Denver hosting 4 of 7, not the 8 hosting because it came from the East.

The evergreen bottleneck is 2 months. The playoffs start mid-April after 82 games, the Finals end mid-June after 16 more wins needed — 4 rounds, up to 28 games for a champion who goes 16-12, so the season is 82 plus 28 = 110 games over 8 months, which is why load management in the 82 exists — the 16 wins in the 2-month playoff matter more than the 50 in the 6-month regular.

Picture this: April-June: Denver as West 1 vs Miami as East 8 after the Play-In — Miami beat Milwaukee as 1 in round 1 4-1, New York 4-2, Boston 4-3 — Denver beat Minnesota 4-1, Phoenix 4-2, LA Lakers 4-0 — Finals Denver hosts 2, Miami hosts 2, Denver hosts 1, Miami hosts 1, Denver hosts 1 — Denver 4-1 over 5 games, 16th win on 12 June, 82 plus 13 playoff wins = 110 games, the 2-month 16-team best-of-7 that rewards the 1 seed's 4 home games.

Myths vs reality

“The NBA playoff is single elimination.”
Reality: Best-of-7 per round, 4 rounds, 16 wins to lift — the 1 vs 8 needs 4 wins, not one, so 8 over 1 is rare vs NCAA's one-game 16 over 1.
“The best regular team wins.”
Reality: The best over 7 games — a 45-win 8 can beat a 60-win 1 in round 1 as Miami did to Milwaukee 2023, but the 7-game still favours the better team 80% over a single game's 65%.
“All playoff teams are top 8.”
Reality: Top 6 are auto, 7-10 play Play-In for 7th and 8th — the 10th can make the 8th spot with two Play-In wins, so 10 of 15 per conference have a shot.

FAQ

How many teams make the NBA playoffs?

16 of 30 — 8 per East and West after the Play-In for 7-10 — then four best-of-7 rounds, 16 wins to lift, over about 2 months.

What is the Play-In?

7 vs 8 for 7th, 9 vs 10 then winner vs loser of 7/8 for 8th — one game each, so 10th can still make 8th.

How long are NBA playoffs?

About 2 months mid-April to mid-June — up to 28 games for the champion who goes 16-12 over 4 rounds.
