How do basketball positions actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Basketball is 5 on court: point guard 1, shooting guard 2, small forward 3, power forward 4, centre 5 — the 1 initiates, the 5 protects the rim, the 3 is the wing who can do both, with modern positionless where the 1-5 is replaced by guard-wing-big but the numbers still set matchups.

Explained properly

Five numbers explain the set. The 1 point guard brings the ball up and calls the set at the top, the 2 shooting guard runs off screens for 3s on the wing, the 3 small forward is the 3-and-D who can guard 2-4, the 4 power forward screens and pops or rolls, the 5 centre anchors the paint with 7-foot rims — the 1-2-3-4-5 is 1m75 to 2m15, which is why the 5 box-outs and the 1 does not, and why the 3's span can guard both.

Why positionless matters is spacing. The 2024 NBA plays 4-out-1 or 5-out with the 5 who shoots 35% from 3 — Jokic at 5, Embiid at 5 — so the 5 drags the rim protector out, which is why the modern 4 is Draymond Green at 6'6" who defends the 5, not a 7-footer — the position is skill, not height, so the 1-5 is now guard-wing-big where the wing can be 2, 3 or 4 by skill.

The FIBA vs NBA explains the 1. The FIBA point 1 shoots more because the three is closer and the lane is wider, so the 1's 3 is more valuable and the 5's post is harder — Tony Parker at 1 and Gobert at 5 show the FIBA 1-5 split vs the NBA's 1-5 spread — the evergreen is the 1's usage rate: NBA 1 at 28%, FIBA 1 at 24%, because the EURO ball movement makes the 3 more willing passer.

The evergreen myth is 5 fixed roles. The modern line is 2 guards, 2 wings, 1 big — the BAL and AfroBasket rosters list 6 guards, 4 wings, 2 bigs, not 2-2-1-2-1 — so the substitution is guard for guard, wing for wing, big for big, and the 3 vs the 2 is decided by wingspan and switchability, not by jersey number, which is why a 6'5" BAL point can be listed as 1 but defend 3.

Picture this: Rwanda BAL: Al Ahly with Ehab Amin at 1, Omot at 3, Ndiaye at 5 — Amin initiates, Omot 3-and-D on the wing, Ndiaye anchor at 5, with positionless where Omot guards the 1-4 — 1 point, 2 shooter, 3 wing, 4 stretch, 5 anchor, not 5 heights but 3 skills guard-wing-big that the BAL sheet now lists as guards-wings-bigs, not 1-5.

Myths vs reality

“Positions are fixed by height.”
Reality: Guard-wing-big by skill — Jokic at 5 shoots 3s, Draymond at 4 defends 5, so the 4 can be 6'6" if he switches, not just 6'10".
“Point guard always dribbles.”
Reality: Positionless — the wing can initiate and the big can bring up, so the 1-5 is now guard-wing-big where the 3 can call the set.
“5 positions means 5 roles.”
Reality: Rosters list 6 guards, 4 wings, 2 bigs — sub is guard-for-guard, wing-for-wing, big-for-big, not 1-for-1.

FAQ

How many positions in basketball?

5: point guard 1, shooting guard 2, small forward 3, power forward 4, centre 5 — modern is guard-wing-big by skill, not height.

What is positionless?

Guard-wing-big where the 5 shoots and the wing initiates — Jokic at 5 brings up and shoots 3s, so the number is skill, not size.

How does BAL list positions?

Guards, wings, bigs — 6+4+2, not 1-5, so the sub is guard for guard, wing for wing.
