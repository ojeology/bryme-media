What is the Australian Open?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The Australian Open is the hard-court Grand Slam in mid-January in Melbourne — 128 singles over 14 days in 35°C heat, best of 5 men, 3 women, with tiebreak 10 at 6-6 final set, heat rule at 4.0 wet bulb, retractable roof on 3 courts, and the Slam where the hard's 30 km/h bounce plus 40°C heat makes the 5-hour semi 40°C court.

Explained properly

The Australian Open is 128 at Melbourne Park 14 days mid-January — 128 men, 128 women, 16 courts 3 roofs (Rod Laver 14,964, Margaret Court 7,500, John Cain 10,500), best-of-5 men, 3 women, 10-point final-set tiebreak at 6-6 as all Slams since 2022 — the hard's acrylic 30 km/h bounce 1.2m at 20°C but 35°C air adds 2 km/h bounce speed and 10% ball fluff wear per hour, which is why 35°C Melbourne mid-January plays faster than Indian Wells 30°C March, and why the heat rule closes the roof at 4.0 wet bulb globe.

The heat rule explains the delay. The AO heat stress scale 1.0-5.0 with wet bulb 32.5°C — at 4.0 the roof closes and play stops 10 minutes to cool, at 5.0 suspension — 2023 had heat rule 3 days, 2014 had 4.0 closure for 3 hours, which is why the 35°C outside is 40°C court with no wind under roof vs SW19's grass 15°C rain roof, and why the night session at 22°C after 19:00 has 70% of ticket revenue vs day's 30% because day is 35°C risk.

The seeding's bottom half matters. The AO draw's 32 seeds as all Slams, live draw halves at Crown — the bottom half's night match at Rod Laver at 19:00 is prime, top half day at 14:00 heat — the 32 seeds split halves, 1 vs 32 round 4, 1 vs 2 final, which is why Djokovic's 10 AO titles came via bottom half 7 times with night matches at 22°C, not top half day heat, and why Sinner's 2024 AO came via top half day heat 35°C not night.

The Happy Slam myth is the crowd. The AO is the Happy Slam for player facilities — $500k appearance for qualifiers plus $105k round 1 — the Happy Slam's 1m attendance over 14 days vs Wimbledon's 500k, with ground pass $60 for outer — the 1m vs 500k attendance plus 35°C vs SW19's 15°C rain explains why the AO's 14 days January is now the richest with $86m prize vs Wimbledon's $65m, which is why the AO's court is hard 30 km/h with roof, not SW19's grass 36 with queue 8mm.

The evergreen is midnight. The AO night session can run to 02:00 with best-of-5 — Murray vs Kokkinakis 04:05 2023, Hewitt vs Baghdatis 04:34 2008 — the latest finish 04:05 with roof open wind 10 km/h under lights vs US Open's 02:26 latest — the 02:00 finish is the AO's hard plus heat's late vs Wimbledon's 21:00 curfew, which is why the AO's 35°C day plus 22°C night makes the 5-hour semi 22:00 still 30°C with roof closed.

Reality: Outdoor hard 30 km/h 1.2m bounce but 35°C air adds 2 km/h vs SW19 15°C — 3 roofs close at 4.0 heat, night session 22°C is the prime, not day heat.

Reality: Wet bulb 4.0 closes roof — 2023 heat rule 3 days at 35°C outside 40°C court — midnight finishes 04:05 under lights, latest of Slams.
