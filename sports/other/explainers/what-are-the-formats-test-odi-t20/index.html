What are Test, ODI and T20 in cricket?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Test is the 5-day, 450-over chess with red ball and white clothes where results can be wins or draws; ODI is the 50-over, 300-ball day game with white ball and coloured shirts where 280 is par; T20 is the 20-over, 120-ball sprint where 170 is par — the same 22-yard pitch, three clocks, three tactics.

Explained properly

Test is 5 days, two innings per side, no over limit per innings — a team can bat 150 overs over two days to make 450, then bowl 90 overs a day to take 20 wickets — the red ball swings more under clouds, the white clothes and lunch and tea breaks pace the day, and the draw exists because time can run out with wickets left, which makes declaration — the captain ending his own innings early to have time to bowl — the Test's unique risk, as when Ben Stokes declares at 250 for a chase.

ODI is 50 overs per side, one innings each in a day — 300 balls, white ball, black sightscreens, coloured shirts, powerplays where only 2-5 fielders are outside the 30-yard circle, so 50 overs has 3 powerplays — 0-10 with 2 outside, 11-40 with 4, 41-50 with 5 — which shapes the tempo: 0-10 hit over the top, 11-40 rotate, 41-50 slog with 6 sixes, where 280-320 is the evergreen par score that 50 overs can produce.

T20 is 20 overs per side, 120 balls, about 85 minutes per innings — the powerplay is 6 overs with 2 outside, then 14 with 5, so the tempo is 6-4-6 — hit 50 in the first 6, rotate 60 in the middle 8, hit 60 in the last 6, where 170 is par, 200 is match-winning and 150 is defendable only with 3 wickets in the powerplay, which is why T20 teams pick 6-7 batters and 4-5 bowlers, not 5 and 5 like Tests.

Why the ball changes is visibility. Test red ball for day, pink for day-night Tests, white for ODIs and T20s under lights — the white swings less than red but is seen under floodlights, so the 50-over and 20-over games are white-ball, day-night or night, while Tests are red-ball day or pink day-night, which is why a pink-ball Test swings at twilight more than a red-ball day test.

The IPL is T20 only, The Hundred is 100 balls per side, not 120, with 10 balls per bowler — the English invention to shorten T20 by 20 balls — and The Ashes is Tests only, 5 Tests over 6 weeks, which is why the format, not the opponent, decides whether a player is a Test specialist like Anderson or a T20 specialist like Russell who barely plays Tests.

Evergreen, the formats select different players. Test picks the batter who can leave the ball for 5 hours and the bowler who can swing the red for 20 overs a day, ODI picks the 360-degree hitter who can rotate and slog, T20 picks the 110mph fast bowler and the 6-hitting finisher who can win in 20 balls — three formats, three squads, one 22-yard pitch.

Picture this: June Test at Lord's: England bat first, make 450 over 150 overs in two days with Root 150, then Australia bat 500 over 170 overs, England bat again 200 over 60 overs, set Australia 150 to win on Day 5, Australia chase 150 in 40 overs with 8 wickets left — 5 days, 450 overs, two innings each, draw possible — while the same two teams at the T20 World Cup play 20 overs each in 3 hours for 180 vs 170 — same rivalry, 150 overs vs 40, draw vs no draw.

Myths vs reality

“Cricket is always 5 days.”
Reality: Test 5 days 450 overs, ODI 50 overs in a day, T20 20 overs in 3 hours — three clocks on the same 22-yard pitch.
“ODI and T20 are the same.”
Reality: ODI 50 overs has 3 powerplays and 280 par, T20 20 overs has 1 powerplay and 170 par — the 30 fewer overs change the batting, not just the time.
“Tests always have a result.”
Reality: Tests can draw after 5 days if time runs out — about 20% do — while ODIs and T20s always produce a winner via Super Over.

FAQ

What is the difference between Test and T20?

Test is 5 days two innings per side, 90 overs per day, red ball, can draw; T20 is 20 overs per side one innings each in 3 hours, white ball, always a winner.

What is par in each format?

Test 300-400 per innings over days, ODI 280-320 in 50 overs, T20 170-190 in 20 overs — the overs, not the pitch, set the par.

What is The Hundred?

England's 100-ball per side invention — 20 balls shorter than T20, 10 balls per bowler, not 24 — to shorten the 3-hour T20 by 20 minutes.
