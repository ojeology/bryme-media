How does the Rugby World Cup work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The Rugby World Cup is 20 nations every 4 years over 7 weeks — 20 play 4 pools of 5 over 4 weeks, top 2 to 8-team single knockout, winner plays 7 games, 48 games total, with 4 points for win plus bonus for 4 tries or losing by 7, not football's 3 points, with the 20th via repechage, not byes.

Explained properly

The Rugby World Cup is 20 from 5 regions via qualifiers plus Next 8: Europe 8, Oceania 4, Americas 3, Africa 1, Asia 1 plus 3 repechage — France 2023 had France host auto plus NZ, SA, England, Wales, Ireland, Australia, Scotland via top 8 2019, the other 12 via regionals — Oceania 1 Fiji plus 2 Samoa/Tonga via playoffs, so the World Cup's 20 includes Namibia's 10th qualification via Africa 1 and Chile's debut via Americas 3 — the 20's repechage Portugal's final win over USA was the 20th, not the 32.

Pools 4x5 is the evergreen. 4 pools of 5 over 4 weeks, 4 games per team — top 2 to quarters single elimination — so pool's 4 games decide the quarter seed: pool winner vs runner of adjacent pool — France pool A 1st vs South Africa pool B 2nd quarter 2023 was 1 vs 2 upset, not 1 vs 1 — the pool 4 of 5 gives the 3rd-placed on 10 points to next World Cup auto, which is why Italy's 3rd in 2023 with 2 wins still auto to 2027 without qualifier, not out like football's 3rd.

Points vs football explains bonus. Win 4, draw 2, plus 1 bonus for 4 tries or losing by 7 or less — bonus can flip 2-1 record's 9 points vs 3-0 on 12 but 1 with 4-try bonus — Scotland beat Tonga with 4 tries bonus 45-17, vs Samoa loss by 7 gets 1 losing bonus 12-14, which is why the 4 pools table's 1st on 15 can be 3 wins plus 3 try bonuses, not 4 wins like football's 9.

The 7-week myth is knockout after pool — 4 weeks pool plus 3 weeks knockout quarter week 5 semis week 6 final week 7 — the 7-week 48 games 5 weeks pool? Actually 40 pool games 4x5x4/2 =40, plus 8 KO =48, not 104 as football 2026 — the 7 weeks 40 pool plus 8 KO vs 39 days 104 for football, which is why the rugby pool's 4 games per team not 3 as football's 12x4, and why yellow is 10 min bin with 14 men while red is 14 men after 20-min replacement since 2023.

The evergreen is Africa 1. The Rugby World Cup's Africa 1 Namibia has never won a World Cup game in 10 appearances, yet qualifies via Africa 1 over Kenya's 20 million — the Africa 1 vs Oceania 4 explains why NZ vs Namibia pool pays the 12m TV for Namibia's 10th try, not the score — the World Cup's 20th Portugal via repechage beating USA shows the 20 not 12 as 1991's 16, so Africa and South America keep a spot like cricket's 10 vs 20, not football's 9 and 6.

Myths vs reality

“Rugby World Cup is 32 teams.”
Reality: 20 teams 4 pools of 5 over 7 weeks — top 2 to 8-team single knockout, 40 pool plus 8 KO =48 games, not 104.
“Win is 3 points.”
Reality: 4 plus 1 bonus for 4 tries or losing by 7 — so a 2-1 with 3 try bonuses can tie 3-0 on table, unlike football's 3 flat.
“Host is neutral.”
Reality: Pool at 9 stadiums across France with host auto — France pool A 1st vs SA B 2nd quarter as 1 vs 2, not 1 vs 1.

FAQ

How many teams at Rugby World Cup?

20 — 4 pools of 5 over 4 weeks, top 2 to 8-team single knockout — 7 weeks 48 games every 4 years.

How many points per win?

4 plus 1 bonus for 4 tries or losing by 7 — so a team can get 2 points from a loss.

Who qualifies for next World Cup?

Pool top 3 auto — 3rd with 2 wins still auto to 2027, like Italy 2023's 2-2 auto, 4th and 5th via regionals.
