How does boxing scoring actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Boxing is 10-point must over 12x3 minute rounds where three judges give 10 to the round winner and 9 or 8 to the loser, plus knockdowns cost a point and fouls cost deductions — a close fight is 115-113, a shutout 120-108, and a draw can be split, majority or unanimous.

Explained properly

The 10-point must is the system. The winner of the round gets 10, the loser 9, or 8 if knocked down or dominated, so 12 rounds means 120-108 is a shutout where every round is 10-9, 115-113 is 7-5 with no knockdowns, and a knockdown round is often 10-8 even if the knocked-down fighter won the rest of the round — the knockdown flips the round, not just the punch.

The judges explain the myth. Three judges sit ringside at different angles, each scores every round on clean punching, effective aggressiveness, ring generalship and defence — clean jabs to the head count more than arm punches to the body, and a flurry that lands 3 of 10 counts less than a single straight that snaps the head — which is why the crowd's cheer for volume often disagrees with the judge's card for clean.

A draw is three types. Unanimous decision is 3-0 on cards, split is 2-1, majority is 2-0 with one draw, and a draw itself can be unanimous 114-114, split 115-113/113-115/114-114, or majority 114-114 twice — the 2023 Fury vs Ngannou split shows how one judge's 10-8 for a knockdown can flip a 114-114 to 115-113 and the result with it.

Knockdowns cost a point automatically — a 10-8 without a warning, two knockdowns in one round is 10-7, which is why a fighter who is knocked down in round 1 and wins 11 of 12 can still draw 113-113 if the knockdown was 10-8 and the 11 wins are 10-9 — the knockdown round is worth double a normal round, which is why a single knockdown in a close fight decides more than 5 close rounds.

Fouls add deductions. Low blow, headbutt or holding can cost 1 point after a warning from the referee, announced as 'point deduction for low blow, Judge 1 9-9' — so a 10-9 round becomes 9-9 and the fighter effectively loses the point he would have won, which is why a close fight with two deductions can be 114-114 even with a 7-5 round split.

Picture this: October title fight: 12 rounds, Champion wins 7 rounds 10-9, Challenger wins 4 rounds 10-9, one round 10-8 for the Champion after a knockdown — Champion 7x10-9 plus 1x10-8 is 115, Challenger 4x10-9 plus the 10-8's 8 is 113, so 115-113 Champion unanimous even though the Challenger won 4 rounds — the 10-8 knockdown decides the card more than the 4 close wins.

Myths vs reality

“The winner gets 10, loser gets nothing.”
Reality: 10-point must — winner 10, loser 9 or 8, so a 12-round shutout is 120-108, a close fight 115-113, and a knockdown round is 10-8.
“Most punches wins the round.”
Reality: Clean effective punching counts more than volume — 3 clean jabs beat 10 arm punches, which is why crowd cheers and judges' cards diverge.
“A draw means even rounds.”
Reality: A draw can be 114-114 unanimous or 115-113/113-115/114-114 split — one judge's 10-8 for a knockdown can flip a draw to a win.

FAQ

How many rounds in a boxing title fight?

12x3 minutes, 10-point must — winner 10, loser 9 or 8, so 120-108 is a shutout, 115-113 is 7-5.

What is a split decision?

2 judges for the winner, 1 for the loser — a 115-113/113-115/114-114 is split, 2-1 on cards.

Does a knockdown always make it 10-8?

Yes, plus a second knockdown makes it 10-7 — the knockdown round is worth double a normal 10-9.
