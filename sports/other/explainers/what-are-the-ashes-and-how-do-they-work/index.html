What are the Ashes — and how do they work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The Ashes is the 5-Test, 25-day Test series between England and Australia played every 2 years over 7 weeks, alternating hosts — 5 Tests of up to 5 days each, 15 sessions per Test, winner keeps the tiny urn said to hold burnt bail from 1882, drawn retains, with no DRS needed in 1882 but now every ball.

Explained properly

The Ashes is 5 Tests over 7 weeks — Test 1 late November in Brisbane or June in Edgbaston, Test 5 early January in Sydney or late July at The Oval — 5 days per Test, 6 hours per day (2 sessions morning-afternoon-evening plus lunch and tea), 15 sessions per Test, 75 sessions for the Ashes, winner 2-2-3? The series winner gets the urn, drawn 2-2 retains to the holder — Australia retained 2-2 in 2023 at The Oval, England needed 3-2 to win, which is why the 2-2 Ashes stays with the holder, not the challenger.

The urn is the myth. The tiny 10cm tall urn at Lord's says the ashes of English cricket burnt and now at Lord's — the 1882 Sporting Times mock obituary after England's loss at The Oval to Australia — the replica goes to the winner, the original stays in the MCC museum, so Stokes and Cummins lift the replica Crystal, not the 1882 clay, which is why the Ashes photo is the replica, not the museum piece.

The sessions explain the war. Five days times 3 sessions — 12.00-13.00 morning, 13.40-15.40 afternoon, 16.00-18.00 evening in England, plus 30 overs per session — the 2023 Ashes second Test at Lord's had 32 wickets in 3 days of swing, which is why the Ashes is not 5 ODIs but 5 five-day campaigns where the Dukes ball swings day 1 and Kookaburra day 2, not white ball with floodlights and 50 overs.

The law twist is Bodyline. The 1932-33 Ashes had England's Bodyline short-pitched at the body to Bradman — the bouncer's fast leg theory — the law changed to 2 bouncers per over, now 2 — the Ashes' 2 is the evergreen law that 2 bouncers over shoulder per over is legal, third is no ball, which is why the fast 90 mph Jofra Archer bouncer in 2019 that hit Smith still counts as one of two, not a foul, and why the Ashes' bouncer count is the umpire's finger, not football's card.

The evergreen is 1882. The Ashes is the oldest trophy in sport contested since 1883 after the 1882 obituary — the only urn where drawn retains — the 2019 series 2-2 Australia retained after 2-2, 2021-22 4-0 Australia at home, which is why the away Ashes is the hardest in sport: Australia has not won in England since 2001, England has not won in Australia since 2010-11, the 7-week 5-Test removes the one-game upset.

Picture this: 2023 Ashes: England vs Australia — 5 Tests: Edgbaston Australia win by 2 wickets, Lord's Australia by 43 runs, Headingley England by 3 wickets, Old Trafford draw rain, The Oval England win — 2-2, Australia retained the urn's replica — 25 days over 7 weeks, 75 sessions, 5 days per Test, Bodyline's 2 bouncers still the law, urn 10cm at Lord's, drawn retains the 1882 ashes.

Myths vs reality

“The Ashes is one game.”
Reality: 5 Tests of 5 days over 7 weeks — 75 sessions, drawn 2-2 retains to holder, so the away team needs 3-2 not 2-2.
“Winners take the urn home.”
Reality: Replica only — original 10cm at Lord's museum since 1882 obituary, replica is the photo with Stokes or Cummins.
“Ashes has no bouncer limit.”
Reality: 2 per over over shoulder — third is no ball — the Bodyline 1932-33 law still governs the 90 mph Archer bouncer.
