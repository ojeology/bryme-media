What are cricket fielding positions?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Cricket has 11 fielders in named positions around the 30-yard circle: slip, gully, point, cover, mid-off, mid-on, square leg, fine leg, third man, with 2 inside the circle mandatory in powerplay, not 9 in baseball, with long-on and deep mid-wicket 70m from the bat for the 6.

Explained properly

The circle plus boundary explain the set. The 11 fielders have 2 wicketkeeper and bowler plus 9 — slips behind keeper for edges, gully square for cuts, point for cuts, cover for drives, mid-off/on for straight, mid-wicket for pulls, square leg for sweeps, fine leg behind legs, third man behind keeper — the long-on and long-off 70m on the boundary for the 6, not the infield's slip/gully 5m — the 9 can be set as 2 slips + gully vs new ball, then 1 slip + cover vs spin.

The powerplay shifts the set. Powerplay 1-10 2 outside means 7 inside — slips 1, point 1, cover 1, mid-off 1, mid-on 1, square leg 1, keeper 1 — not long-on 70m — the death 41-50 with 5 outside means long-on, long-off, deep mid-wicket, deep cover, third man 5 on the rope for the 6, which is why the ODIs 320 sets the field: powerplay 1-10 with long-on hidden 7 inside, death with 5 long on the rope.

The slip cordon explains the new ball. First 10 overs with Dukes swing have 3 slips and gully 4 behind the bat for edges at 85 mph — the slip 1m from keeper, gully 5m — the cordon catches 30% of wickets in the powerplay vs 10% after, which is why the Ashes' first 10 overs have 3 slips and the IPL's white ball with no swing after 2 overs has 1 slip, not 3, and why the 3-slip cordon is the Ashes' 15-session, not ODI's 10-over, set.

The leg trap is the myth. Fine leg deep behind square vs sweep, square leg for the pull, short leg under the helmet for bat-pad vs spin — the 11 cannot be 6 on leg before the ball (2 behind square max), which is why the leg trap's 5 on leg is fine leg + short leg + square leg + mid-wicket + mid-on, not 6 behind square — the law caps 2 behind square on leg, so the field's 11 is not open like baseball's 9.

The evergreen is off vs leg. Off side is where the bat faces, leg is behind — the field is set off-heavy vs right-hander with 7 on off, leg with 4, vs switch-hit the off retains — the Ashes' right-arm over to right-hander's off is the 3-slip cordon off, not leg, which is why the off's cover 20m and point 25m are the drive's catchers, not the leg's sweep.

Picture this: ODI powerplay 1-10: 2 outside so slips 1, gully 1, point 1, cover 1, mid-off/on, square leg — death 41-50: 5 outside long-on 70m, long-off, deep mid-wicket, deep cover, third man — Ashes new ball 3 slips plus gully 4 behind for Dukes swing vs IPL white after 2 overs 1 slip — the 11's 9 minus keeper/bowler set as off vs leg, not 9 infield as baseball's 9.

Myths vs reality

“Cricket field is like baseball.”
Reality: 11 fielders named slip, gully, point, cover, mid-off/on, square leg, fine leg, third man — 2 slips plus gully vs new ball, 5 long on rope death.
“Powerplay field is open.”
Reality: Powerplay 1-10 2 outside =7 inside — 3 slips not long-on 70m — death 5 outside =5 long on rope for the 6.
“Leg can have 6.”
Reality: Max 2 behind square on leg — short leg + fine leg behind square capped, so 5 on leg not 6.

FAQ

How many fielders in cricket?

11 — 9 plus keeper and bowler — slip, gully, point, cover, mid-off/on, square leg, fine leg, third man, long-on 70m.

What is slip cordon?

3 slips behind keeper for edges vs new ball swing — 30% of wickets powerplay, 1 slip later vs spin.

How many on leg?

Max 2 behind square on leg — fine leg + short leg capped, so 5 on leg total, not 6.
