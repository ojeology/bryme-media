What is MMA — and how does the UFC work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: MMA is mixed martial arts where a fight can be won by knockout, submission or judges' 10-point must over 3x5 minute rounds (5x5 for titles), and the UFC is the top promotion with weight classes from strawweight to heavyweight, a pound-for-pound ranking, and the champion who holds the belt until beaten or stripped.

Explained properly

MMA blends three bases. Striking from boxing and kickboxing, wrestling for takedowns and control, and jiu-jitsu for submissions — a fighter can win on the feet by knockout at 70% of finishes, on the ground by submission like rear-naked choke at 20%, or on the cards if it goes the distance — which is why a wrestler who controls 10 of 15 minutes on the ground can beat a striker who lands more but is held, and why the style matchup decides more than the record.

The UFC's weight classes are 8 men's and 4 women's, each 10-15 lbs apart — flyweight 125, bantamweight 135, featherweight 145, lightweight 155, welterweight 170, middleweight 185, light heavyweight 205, heavyweight 265 — with a one-pound allowance for non-title fights, so a 170.5 walk-around can make 170, but a 171 cannot without a catchweight, which is why weight cuts of 15-20 lbs in 48 hours are the evergreen health myth.

The rounds explain the scoring. Three rounds 5 minutes for non-title, 5x5 for championship and main events, 10-point must per round — 10-9 for the winner, 10-8 for dominance — but a 10-8 is rarer than boxing because a dominant round with a near-finish is still 10-9 unless the loser is saved by the bell, which is why 29-28 can be 2 rounds to 1 and 30-27 is a shutout, and why a fighter who wins 2 of 3 but is knocked down in the 2nd can still lose 28-28 on that round's 10-8.

Why the UFC dominates is the single promotion. The UFC owns the top 15 per weight class in its ranking, matchmakes champion vs interim, and strips for inactivity — the belt, not a sanctioning body, is the title, so the UFC champion is the world champion in practice, which is why a Bellator or PFL champion who moves to the UFC often enters at 8-10 and must win 2 to challenge, not unify.

The evergreen myth is the cage. The Octagon is 30 feet across with an 8-sided fence, not a ring, so the fence can be used to stand up from a takedown — the wall-walk — and a fighter who grabs the fence loses a point, which is why the cage favours wrestlers who can drive to the fence and strikers who can sprawl, not just boxers who would be trapped in a ring's corner.

Picture this: July title at lightweight 155: Champion 5x5 vs Challenger 3x5 path — Champion wins rounds 1-2 10-9, Challenger knocks down in round 3 for 10-8, Champion wins 4 10-9, Challenger wins 5 10-9 — 48-46 Champion, 2 rounds lost but the 10-8 third keeps the challenger close, which is why a 3-2 on rounds can be 48-46 not 49-46 — the knockdown round is the swing that boxing also has, but with 5 rounds not 12.

Myths vs reality

“MMA and UFC are the same.”
Reality: MMA is the sport, UFC is the top promotion — Bellator, PFL and ONE are other promotions where the UFC champion is treated as the world champion.
“Any weight can fight any weight.”
Reality: Eight men's classes 125-265 with 10-15 lbs gaps — a 170 cannot fight at 185 without missing weight and a catchweight, and 265 is the heavyweight cap.
“UFC scoring is like boxing's 120-108.”
Reality: 10-point must per 5-minute round, but only 3x5 or 5x5 rounds, so 30-27 is a 3-0 shutout and a 10-8 round is rarer than boxing's.

FAQ

How long is a UFC fight?

3x5 minutes, 5x5 for titles and main events — 15 or 25 minutes, 10-point must per round, no draw unless even.

How many weight classes in the UFC?

8 men's 125-265 and 4 women's 115-145, each about 10-15 lbs apart — a fighter cuts 15-20 lbs to make weight in 48 hours.

What is the Octagon?

The 30-foot 8-sided cage — not a ring — where the fence can be used to stand up, and grabbing it costs a point.
