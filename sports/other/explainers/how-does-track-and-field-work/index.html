How does track and field actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Track and field is the Olympics' central sport — track is timed races from 100m to 10,000m, field is measured jumps and throws, combined events are heptathlon and decathlon over two days, and every event has heats, semis and a final where 0.01 seconds and 1cm decide, not points.

Explained properly

Track is lanes and times. The 100m and 110m hurdles are straight-lane 10-second races with 8 lanes, the 200m and 400m are lane all the way, the 800m is one bend in lanes then cut in, the 1500m to 10,000m are pack races with no lanes after the start — the 100m is 10 seconds, the marathon is 42.195km at about 2 hours, and the 400m hurdles add 10 barriers, which is why the 400h world record is 45.94 for men.

Field is distance, not time. High jump and pole vault are height over a bar with 3 failures ends it, long and triple jump are distance from the board, shot put, discus, hammer and javelin are distance from the circle — the discus 2kg for men flies 70m, the javelin 800g flies 90m, and the hammer 7.26kg spins 80m, so the throw is technique plus speed, not just strength.

Heptathlon and decathlon are the two-day combined. Heptathlon is 7 over two days for women, decathlon 10 for men — points per performance on a table, not place, so a 11.00 100m is 861 points and a 7.50 long jump is 945, and the winner is highest total after the last event (800m for heptathlon, 1500m for decathlon), which is why the 1500m in the decathlon is run by exhausted athletes but decides gold.

Rounds explain the week. The 100m has heats, semis and a final over two days — 48 in heats, 24 in semis, 8 in the final — while the marathon is one final, and the field has qualification then final — 32 in qual, 12 in final with 6 attempts, then top 8 get 3 more — so the schedule, not just the distance, decides who peaks — heats at 10am, final at 9pm under lights.

Records are the evergreen. The men's 100m world record is 9.58 by Bolt, the women's 10.49 by Griffith-Joyner, the marathon 2:00:35 by Kiptum — barriers not laws set them, so each record is a 0.01 shave, not a rule change like football's offside, which is why the 9.58 is still the 100m myth that 9.60 still chases 15 years later.

Picture this: Olympic final 2024: Men's 100m — 8 lanes, 9.79 winning with 0.01 between 1st and 2nd, decathlon — 10 events over two days, 8750 points wins, field — high jump bar at 2.36m for gold on third attempt, javelin 89m for gold — same stadium, lanes for time, pit for distance, table for combined, not a single table like football.

Myths vs reality

“Track is just running.”
Reality: Track is timed lanes 100m to 10,000m, field is measured jumps and throws, combined is points over 7 or 10 events in two days — three sports in one stadium.
“All track races are lanes.”
Reality: 100m and hurdles are lane, 200m and 400m lane all the way, 800m one bend then pack, 1500m+ pack from the start.
“Fastest time always wins the combined.”
Reality: Combined is points per performance on a table — 11.00 100m is 861, 7.50 long jump 945, so the 1500m last can flip the total even if the leader is slower.

FAQ

How many events in the decathlon?

10 over two days — 100m, long jump, shot, high jump, 400m day one, hurdles, discus, pole vault, javelin, 1500m day two.

How many in the 100m final?

8 lanes — heats 48, semis 24, final 8, with 0.01 deciding, not a table.

What is the 100m world record?

9.58 Bolt men, 10.49 Griffith-Joyner women — 15 years unbroken for Bolt, the evergreen barrier.
