How does the NBA Draft lottery actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The NBA Draft lottery is 14 balls for 14 non-playoff teams, 1,001 combinations, where the worst has 14% for 1st and cannot fall past 5th — the lottery decides the top 4 picks, the rest order by record, with no team owning the worst record guarantee for 1st since 2019 flattening.

Explained properly

The lottery is 14 balls numbered 1-14, drawn 4 at a time for 1,001 combos (one discarded) — the 30-52 worst gets 140 combos = 14.0%, the 29-53 second gets 14.0%, the 28-54 third gets 14.0%, the fourth about 12.5%, so the worst three each 14% flat since 2019, not the old 25% for worst, which is why the 30-win team can fall to 5th and the 5th-worst can jump to 1st.

Top 4 are lottery, 5-14 are record. The 4 draws decide picks 1-4, then 5-14 is inverse record, so the worst cannot fall past 5th but can be 5th if 4 teams jump over — Detroit at 14-68 in 2024 kept odds 14% but fell to 5th when Atlanta 5th-worst jumped to 1st, which is why the 14% is not a guarantee of top 4, only top 5, and why the play-in losers at 9-10 still enter the 14 as the best odds of the lottery.

The flattening explains tanking. Before 2019 worst was 25% and could not fall past 4th, so 20 teams tanked; after 2019 worst three 14% flat and can fall to 5th, so the incentive to be worst is cut 11%, which is why 2023 Victor Wembanyama went to San Antonio 5th-worst at 12.5% not Detroit worst — the 14% flat made the 5th-worst's odds almost equal to the worst's, which is why the lottery broadcast now needs the envelope, not the record.

The evergreen myth is worst always 1st. Worst 14% means 86% not 1st — the 2023 San Antonio 5th-worst 12.5% win over Detroit's 14% shows the 1.5% flat gap matters more than the 1 game gap, and why the Second Spectrum's 14-ball machine draws 20 seconds per pick, not the record, and why the BAL and AfroBasket have no lottery — the talent is drafted, not transferred, which is why the WNBA vs NBA lottery differs in odds.

Picture this: May lottery: 14 balls, 14 teams, 14% worst three flat — Detroit worst falls to 5th in 2024, Atlanta 5th-worst jumps to 1st at 10.5% — 1,001 combos, top 4 lottery then 5-14 by record — the 2019 flattening that cut worst from 25% to 14% and lets 5th jump over worst with 1.5% gap, unlike the WNBA's 25% for worst or the G-League's tryout.

Myths vs reality

“Worst team gets 1st.”
Reality: 14% flat for worst three since 2019 — worst can fall to 5th and 5th-worst can jump to 1st, as 2024 Detroit 14-68 fell to 5th.
“All 14 picks are lottery.”
Reality: Lottery decides top 4 only, 5-14 by inverse record — the worst never falls past 5th, but 5th can be 1st.
“Lottery is record-based.”
Reality: 14 balls 1-14 drawn 4 for 1,001 combos — worst gets 140 combos =14%, second worst 140, so one ball can flip 1st.

FAQ

What are odds for worst in NBA lottery?

14% flat for worst three since 2019 — worst cannot fall past 5th, but 5th-worst also 10.5% and can jump to 1st.

How many picks are lottery?

Top 4 via 14 balls 1,001 combos, then 5-14 by record — the envelope, not the record, decides 1-4.

When did lottery flatten?

2019 — worst from 25% to 14% flat for three, to disincentivise tanking — worst has not won since 2018.
