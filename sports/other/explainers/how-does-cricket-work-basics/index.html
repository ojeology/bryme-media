How does cricket actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Cricket is 11-a-side where one team bats to score runs by hitting the ball and running between two wickets 20.12m apart, the other bowls to get 10 batters out, and the side with more runs wins — the three formats are Test over 5 days with 90 overs per day, ODI over 50 overs per side in a day, and T20 over 20 overs in 3 hours.

Explained properly

The wicket explains the target. Two stumps with bails at each end 20.12m apart — the bowler bowls from one end to the batter at the other who protects the stumps, and a run is scored when the two batters swap ends after hitting, or 4 if the ball reaches the boundary on the bounce, 6 if it clears on the full. Ten of 11 must be out to end an innings — the not-out batter is stranded, which is why 10 down is all out even with 11.

Bowling explains the defence. The bowler must release with a straight arm, bounce the ball or bowl full, and get the batter out in 10 ways — bowled (hit stumps), lbw (would have hit), caught (hit and caught), run out (stumps hit while running) are the 90% — then the next batter comes in. An over is 6 legal balls, 90 overs per day in Tests, 50 per side in ODIs, 20 in T20s, so a Test innings can be 150 overs over two days while a T20 innings is 20 overs in 85 minutes.

Pitch explains condition. The 22-yard pitch is clay and grass that cracks, so a Day 1 green pitch seams and swings for fast bowlers at 85-90 mph, a Day 4 dry pitch spins for spinners who turn the ball sideways, and a flat pitch does neither — the toss winner often bats if green and bowls if dry, which is why the pitch report before the toss decides the game as much as the players.

Why no draw in white ball is format. Tests can draw after 5 days if time runs out with wickets left — about 20% do — while ODIs and T20s always produce a winner via Super Over if level — one over per side, then second, then boundary count was scrapped after 2019's England vs New Zealand tie where the Super Over also tied and England won on boundaries, now unlimited Super Overs until a winner.

The IPL explains the money. The Indian Premier League is T20 franchise cricket — 10 teams, March-May, $6bn TV deal, players auctioned — where a 20-over innings with 6 sixes and 10 fours can be 200 runs, and a chase of 200 in 20 overs is a par, so the 5-day Test's patience and the 3-hour T20's hitting are the same sport at opposite tempos.

Picture this: At the 2023 ODI World Cup final in Ahmedabad: Australia bowl first on a dry pitch, India bat and are 240 all out in 50 overs — 240 off 300 balls with 25 fours and 5 sixes — Australia chase 241 off 300 balls in 43 overs with Travis Head scoring 137, 15 fours and 4 sixes, and win by 6 wickets with 7 overs spare — 50 overs per side, 10 down, more runs wins, the ODI format in one scorecard.

Myths vs reality

“Cricket is one format.”
Reality: Test 5 days 90 overs per day, ODI 50 overs per side in a day, T20 20 overs in 3 hours — same wicket 20.12m apart, three clocks.
“All 11 must be out.”
Reality: 10 down is all out — the not-out batter is stranded with no partner, so 10 is the innings end even with 11.
“A tie is a draw.”
Reality: Only Tests can draw on time. ODIs and T20s go to Super Overs until a winner — unlimited since 2019's boundary-count controversy.

FAQ

How long is a cricket game?

Test up to 5 days (90 overs per day), ODI about 8 hours (50 overs per side), T20 about 3 hours (20 overs per side).

How do you score runs?

Hit, run 20.12m between wickets for 1, or 4 if the ball reaches the boundary on the bounce, 6 if it clears on the full.

How is a batter out?

Ten ways — bowled, lbw, caught and run out are 90% — 10 down is all out even with 11 batters.
