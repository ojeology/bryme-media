What is the Tour de France?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The Tour de France is cycling's 21-stage, 3-week Grand Tour over about 3,400km in July — 8 riders per team, 22 teams, yellow for the overall leader by time, green for sprint points, polka dot for mountain points, white for best young, with the winner decided by total time, not stage wins.

Explained properly

The Tour is 21 stages over 23 days with two rest days, about 3,400km — 7 flat stages for sprinters at 50 km/h, 6 mountain stages in the Pyrenees and Alps with 2,000m climbs like Alpe d'Huez, 6 hilly medium, and 2 time trials where each rider races alone against the clock. The yellow jersey is the lowest total time — not most wins — so a rider who wins no stage can win the Tour if he is 2nd on every mountain, which happened in 1968.

The jerseys explain the parallel races. Yellow is overall time, green is sprint points at intermediate sprints and stage finishes, polka dot is mountain points at summits by category — hors catégorie highest — white is best under-26 by time, so one stage has four competitions: the break takes the polka, the sprinter takes green, the GC favourite protects yellow, the young rider attacks for white, all in the same 180km road.

The team explains the win. Twenty-two teams of 8 — 18 WorldTour plus 4 wildcard — ride as a train: domestiques fetch bottles, set tempo on the climb at 20 km/h to drop rivals, and the leader sits in the wheels at 30% less wind until the last 2km, so the Tour winner is the strongest who also has the strongest team — the leader's 7 helpers can be the difference between keeping yellow by 8 seconds or losing it on the Ventoux.

Time gaps decide more than stages. A flat stage win is often 0 seconds gap — the bunch finishes with the same time — while a mountain stage can create 2 minutes — the GC is won in the 3 mountain stages where gaps open, not the 7 flats where the bunch finishes together, which is why a sprinter can win 5 stages and be 100th overall, and a GC rider like Pogacar can win the Tour with 3 stage wins.

The evergreen myth is doping. The Tour's EPO era 1990s-2006 saw 7 Armstrong titles stripped, so the post-2010 Tour added the biological passport, where blood values are tracked longitudinally and a 2% reticulocyte swing triggers a target test — the 21-stage 3,400km is now the most tested event in sport, which is why the 2020-24 winners are seen as the clean generation, not the 2000s'.

Women's explains the gap. La Tour Femmes is 8 stages over 8 days in July after the men's, not 21 — the women's Yellow also by time, but over 950km, which started in 2022 after the old 14-stage version folded, so the men's Tour's 21-stage 3,400km remains the longest Grand Tour vs the Giro and Vuelta at 21 stages but about 3,200km.

Picture this: July: Stage 17 to Courchevel — 165km, 5,000m climbing, break goes at km 10, polka leader takes 20 points on the Madeleine, Pogacar attacks on the last 7km at 20 km/h, gains 45 seconds on Vingegaard, yellow changes shoulders by 8 seconds, green sprinter finishes 25 minutes down but keeps green, 8 riders per team, 22 teams, yellow by total time, not the stage win.

Myths vs reality

“The Tour is one race.”
Reality: 21 stages over 3 weeks — 7 flat, 6 mountain, 6 hilly, 2 time trials, 3,400km — yellow by total time, not most stage wins.
“The yellow wins the most stages.”
Reality: Yellow is lowest total time — a rider can win no stage and win the Tour if he is 2nd on every mountain, as in 1968.
“Cycling is individual.”
Reality: 8 per team — domestiques set tempo, fetch bottles, and shield the leader at 30% less wind until the last 2km, so the team wins the Tour.

FAQ

How long is the Tour de France?

21 stages over 23 days with 2 rest days, about 3,400km — 7 flat, 6 mountain, 6 hilly, 2 time trials, yellow by total time.

What are the jerseys?

Yellow overall time, green sprint points, polka dot mountain points, white best young — four races in one peloton.

Is the women's Tour the same?

La Tour Femmes is 8 stages over 8 days, not 21 — started 2022, same jerseys, 950km, not 3,400.
