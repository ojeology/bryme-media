How does golf actually work — and what are the four majors?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Golf is 18 holes, lowest strokes wins, with the four majors — Masters at Augusta in April, PGA Championship in May, US Open in June, The Open at links in July — each 156 players over 4 rounds of 18 holes, 72 holes total, cut after 36 holes to top 65 and ties, and the winner gets the green jacket, Wanamaker, or Claret Jug.

Explained properly

The 18 holes explain par. Each hole has par 3, 4 or 5 — par 4 is 380-450 yards to the hole in 2 shots plus 2 putts, par 5 is 530+ with 3 shots, par 3 is 180-220 in 1 shot — the course par is 70-72 over 18 holes, so 4 rounds par is 280-288, and the winner is the lowest over 72 holes, not the most holes won, which is why a 65 in round 1 and a 75 in round 4 can still be 280 total.

The four majors are separate owners. The Masters is Augusta National's invitational 88-90 players based on past champions and world ranking, the PGA is the PGA of America's 156, the US Open is the USGA's 156 with qualifying, The Open is the R&A's 156 links — the majors share no tour, so a LIV player like Koepka can play the majors via the qualifiers but not the regular PGA Tour's FedEx events, which is why the majors are the only place LIV and PGA meet.

The cut explains the weekend. After 36 holes (two rounds) the field cuts to top 65 and ties — about half go home Friday night — so the weekend is 70-85 players over 36 holes, with the leader teeing last on Sunday at about 14:30 local. The cut at 4 over can keep a 3-time major winner in while a world number 5 on 5 over goes home, which is why the Thursday-Friday grind is survival, not just score.

Why links matters is wind. The Open at St Andrews or Royal Liverpool is links — firm, treeless, wind 15-25 mph — so the ball bounces 40 yards and pot bunkers trap, while the Masters at Augusta is parkland with soft greens that stop, so the same 7-iron is 150 yards at Augusta with spin and 130 at St Andrews with wind, which is why a Masters winner often misses the Open cut and the Open winner often misses the Masters cut.

The evergreen bottleneck is 72 holes. A 3-shot lead with 6 holes left is about 70% to win, but the 18th at Augusta or the 17th at Sawgrass can flip it — a double bogey 6 on a par 4 moves -10 to -8 and the chaser's 66 from -8 to -10 wins, which is why final-round 65s like Nick Faldo's 65 to win the 1996 Masters from 6 back are the myth that 6 back with 18 left is dead, not alive.

Picture this: April Masters: 89 players, cut after 36 holes to 50, leader -8 after 54 holes, final round 18 holes Sunday, winner -12 over 72 holes with a 68-70-69-65, green jacket at Butler Cabin, then the Open in July at Royal Liverpool 156 players links wind 20 mph, winner -9 over 72, the same 72-hole total but parkland vs links, August vs April, August the Claret Jug.

Myths vs reality

“Golf is one tournament.”
Reality: Four majors 156 players over 72 holes each — Masters, PGA, US Open, The Open — plus the regular Tour's 40 events that fund them.
“Lowest score one day wins.”
Reality: Lowest over 72 holes — 4 rounds, cut after 36 to top 65 and ties, so two bad holes on Friday can still miss the weekend even with a Thursday 65.
“The Masters is open to all.”
Reality: Invitational 88-90 — past champions, top 50 in world, amateurs — not open qualifying like the US Open's 156 via qualifying.

FAQ

What are the four golf majors?

Masters Augusta April, PGA May, US Open June, The Open July — 156 players (Masters 89), 72 holes over 4 rounds, cut after 36 to top 65 and ties.

How long is a major?

72 holes over 4 days — 18 per day, 4 rounds, about 12 hours of golf, lowest strokes over 72 wins.

What is the cut?

After 36 holes (Friday), top 65 and ties go to the weekend — about half go home, so 65-85 play the last 36 holes.
