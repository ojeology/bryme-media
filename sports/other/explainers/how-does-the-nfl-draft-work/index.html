How does the NFL Draft actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The NFL Draft is 7 rounds, 259 picks over 3 days each April, where the worst teams pick first and the champion picks last, with the first pick signing for about $40m over 4 years and the last pick Mr Irrelevant getting a parade — the draft, not transfers, is how American football distributes talent.

Explained properly

The Draft is worst-first for parity. The 17-game regular season decides the order — 32 teams, 4-13 last picks 1st, 14-3 champion picks 32nd, with no relegation to reward losing — so the 1st pick can be a franchise quarterback like Caleb Williams at $39m over 4, and the 259th Mr Irrelevant is Brock Purdy who went from last pick to Super Bowl starter, which is why losing is not punished with a drop but rewarded with the top pick.

The 7 rounds explain depth. Round 1 is stars, round 2 starters, rounds 3-4 rotation, rounds 5-7 projects and special teams — the 259 picks are 32 per round plus 32 compensatory picks for lost free agents, so a team that loses 4 free agents can get 4 extra picks in rounds 3-7, which is why the draft is 259, not 224, and why a comp pick in round 3 can be worth a second-round talent.

Trades make the evergreen. Picks are currency — a team can trade 1st this year for 1st and 2nd next year, or trade a player for a 1st, so the draft board moves live — the 2023 move of the 1st for four picks and DJ Moore shows the price — and the clock is 10 minutes in round 1, 7 in round 2, 5 in 3-6, 4 in round 7, so the war room has 10 minutes to decide the franchise's next 4 years.

Why no transfer fees is the cap. The NFL salary cap is about $255m per team in 2024 with a hard cap and a fifth-year option for round 1, so a star cannot be bought for cash like football — he is drafted, paid on the rookie wage scale, and after 4 years his fifth-year option or franchise tag keeps him, which is why the Draft is the only entry point.

The undrafted explains the rest. About 500 players who go undrafted sign as free agents minutes after the 259th — Mr Irrelevant gets the parade in Newport Beach, but the undrafted free agent who makes the 53-man roster after camp gets the same $750k minimum as the 7th rounder, so the Draft is the main door, not the only door.

Picture this: April in Detroit: Chicago at 7-10 picks 1st and takes Caleb Williams QB USC for $39m over 4, Washington at 4-13 picks 2nd and takes Jayden Daniels, Chicago again at 9-8 picks 9th via a trade, and with the 259th Mr Irrelevant the 49ers took Brock Purdy at $3.7m over 4 who started the 2023 Super Bowl — worst-first 259 picks, 3 days, 10-minute clock in round 1, the inverse of football's open market.

Myths vs reality

“The NFL Draft is like football transfers.”
Reality: Worst picks first, 7 rounds, 259 picks, no cash transfer fees — the draft is how talent is distributed, not the market, with a rookie wage scale.
“The last pick is worthless.”
Reality: Mr Irrelevant 259th gets a 4-year deal and a parade — Brock Purdy from 259th started a Super Bowl, so the last pick can beat the first.
“Draft order is random.”
Reality: Worst-first from the 17-game season — 4-13 picks 1st, 14-3 champion picks 32nd — with 32 compensatory picks for lost free agents making 259, not 224.

FAQ

How many picks in the NFL Draft?

259 over 7 rounds — 32 per round plus 32 compensatory picks for lost free agents — over 3 days in April, worst-first.

Who is Mr Irrelevant?

The 259th last pick — gets a parade in Newport Beach and a 4-year deal, famously Brock Purdy who went from 259th to Super Bowl starter.

Can picks be traded?

Yes, picks are currency — 1st this year for 1st and 2nd next year plus a player is common, with 10 minutes on the clock in round 1.
