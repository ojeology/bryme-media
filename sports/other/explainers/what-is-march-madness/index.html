What is March Madness?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: March Madness is the NCAA Division I men's 68-team single-elimination bracket over 3 weeks in March — 68 play in a 4-game First Four, then 64 play 6 rounds to the Final Four, with one-game upsets where a 15 seed beats a 2 about 7% and the champion plays 6 games, not 82.

Explained properly

March Madness is 68, not 64 — the field is 68 with 4 First Four play-ins in Dayton: 4 at-large bubble 11 seeds and 4 lowest 16 seeds play for the 64, so the 64 proper starts with 32 first-round games, then 32 second, 16 Sweet 16, 8 Elite 8, 4 Final Four, 2 National semis and 1 title — the bracket is 67 games over 21 days, not 380 over 9 months like football.

Single elimination explains the mad. One game per team per round, no series, so the better team wins about 65% of single games vs 80% of NBA best-of-7, which is why 15 over 2 happens about 7% — Saint Peter's 15 over Kentucky 2 in 2022, Oral Roberts 15 over Ohio State 2021 — the single game makes the 2's 80% fall to 65%, which is why the 1s have never lost to a 16 until UMBC 16 over Virginia 1 in 2018, 136-1.

The seeding explains the region. Four regions of 17 — East, West, South, Midwest — each with 1-16 plus First Four feed, 1 plays 16, 2 plays 15, 8 plays 9, so the early upset is a 10 over 7 in the 7 vs 10 game, which is not an upset by power but by seed, and why the First Four's 11 vs 11 for the 11 seed can then beat the 6 in the first round as 11 is actually an at-large, not a mid-major.

The money is the gate, not the pay. Players until 2021 were amateur, not paid, but the NCAA sells the 67 games to CBS/Turner for $1bn per year, and the unit system pays conferences per game played — a unit is about $2m over 6 years, so a 15 seed's one win earns its conference $2m — the coach's $5m bonus is not the player's, which is why the transfer portal and NIL now let the player earn name-image-likeness but not the unit.

The Final Four is the evergreen neutral. Two semis Saturday, final Monday on a football stadium floor with 70,000 raised in Indianapolis, New Orleans or Houston — not a campus — so the home court is neutral, which is why Duke's Cameron 9,000 noise never appears in March and why the 1 vs 4 Final Four is often 2one-and-dones vs 2 seniors, not just talent.

Picture this: Dayton to Houston: Fairleigh Dickinson 16 from the First Four beats Purdue 1 as 16 over 1 63-58 in the 2023 first round — 68 to 64 in Dayton, 6 rounds in 21 days, Saint Peter's 15 to the Elite 8 in 2022 — the 67-game single-elimination bracket where the 15 over 2 is 7% and the 16 over 1 was 0% until 2018, funded by $1bn and the unit's $2m per win.

Myths vs reality

“March Madness is 64 teams.”
Reality: 68 — 4 First Four in Dayton feed the 64, then 64 play 6 rounds over 21 days, 67 games total to the single Final Four.
“Best team always wins.”
Reality: Single game — better wins 65% single vs 80% best-of-7 — so 15 over 2 is 7% and 16 over 1 was 136-1 until UMBC 2018.
“Players are paid.”
Reality: Until 2021 no, now NIL only — the $1bn TV goes to schools via units $2m per game over 6 years, not salaries.

FAQ

How many teams in March Madness?

68 — 4 First Four in Dayton feed the 64, then 6 rounds over 21 days, 67 games to the Final Four.

Can a 15 beat a 2?

Yes about 7% — Saint Peter's 15 over Kentucky 2 in 2022 was 15 over 2, the 16 over 1 was 0 until UMBC 16 over Virginia 1 in 2018.

What is a unit?

The NCAA payout per tournament game — about $2m over 6 years to the conference, so a 15 seed's upset earns $2m.
