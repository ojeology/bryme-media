What is Wimbledon and how does it work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Wimbledon is the grass Grand Slam in early July at SW19 London — 128 singles, best of 5 for men, 3 for women, over 14 days with white clothing, no Hawkeye until 2007, no tiebreak until 2019, queue instead of ballot, and the Ballon d'Or of tennis where the serve wins 60% on grass vs 42% on clay.

Explained properly

Wimbledon is 128 over 14 days — 128 men's singles, 128 women's, best of 5 for men (first to 3 sets, 6 games per set with tiebreak 7 at 6-6, final set 10 at 6-6 since 2022), best of 3 for women, doubles same, with the middle Sunday now day 7 since 2022 after the rain-less roof era — the queue outside SW19 coaches for 1,500 ground passes at £27 vs debenture £80k for Centre 1,500 seats, which is why the ballot's 150k applicants compete for 41,500 capacity over 14 days, not just screen.

Grass explains success. The 100% rye at 8mm cut daily to 8mm gives low bounce 1.0m at 36 km/h after, serve wins 60% on grass vs 54% hard 42% clay, so the ace's 10 per match vs 5 on clay decides — the 36 km/h low bounce on rye at SW19 at 15°C with roof closed adds 2 mph vs open 20°C wind, which is why the roof's 10 minute close with 30 km/h wind off saves the final's 6pm light vs Roland Garros's clay 22°C open's 24 km/h high bounce.

The clothing is the myth. All white with 10mm colour trim since 1887 — the white's 10mm is measured, cream fails the test — the 2023 easing allows dark undershorts for women, not coloured dress — the 10mm white is the evergreen that costs the player $500 for a non-white shoe's red sole, which is why Kyrgios' red Air Jordan was banned in 2022 while his white one passed, and why the queue's white dress code still fines the junior's 11mm trim.

The tiebreak's 2019 ended endless. Wimbledon had no tiebreak final set before 2019, so Isner-Mahut 70-68 11 hours over 3 days 2010's final set 70-68 at 70 games — since 2019 at 12-12 final-set tiebreak, since 2022 at 6-6 final-set 10 by 2 — the 10-point at 6-6 10-7 replaces the 70-68, which is why the 2023 Alcaraz-Djokovic 1-6,7-6(6),6-1,3-6,6-4 had no 10-point final but 6-4, not 10-7, because it ended before 6-6, not after as 2019 would have demanded.

The queue vs ballot explains tickets. The ballot is September lottery for 50% of tickets, the queue is overnight camp with numbered queue card for ground pass £27 day 1 for Court 3, debenture is 5-year £80k for Centre's 1,500 seats sold via corporate, which is why the queue's 10:00 line at Wimbledon Park 7am with 8-hour wait still beats StubHub's £5,000 Centre final vs debenture holder's resale, and why SW19 has 18 courts not 1.

Picture this: Wimbledon 2023 Centre: SW19 14 days July — 128 men best-of-5, women best-of-3 — grass 1.0m 36 km/h vs clay 1.5m 24 — Alcaraz vs Djokovic final 1-6,7-6(6),6-1,3-6,6-4 over 4:42, final set 6-4 not 10-7 as 6-4 before 6-6 — All white 10mm, queue 1,500 ground £27 vs debenture £80k, Isner-Mahut 70-68 before 2019's 12-12 tiebreak.

Myths vs reality

“Wimbledon is hard court.”
Reality: 100% rye 8mm grass at SW19 — 36 km/h low bounce 1.0m, serve wins 60% vs 42% on clay — the grass that 14-day queue's ground pass £27 still sees.
“Any colour is allowed.”
Reality: All white 10mm trim — cream fails, red sole banned 2022 — the 10mm white dress code still fines non-white shoes.
“No tiebreak at Wimbledon.”
Reality: Since 2019 at 12-12 tiebreak, since 2022 at 6-6 final-set 10 by 2 — Isner-Mahut 70-68 2010 will never return.

FAQ

Early July SW19 London — 128 singles over 14 days, men best-of-5, women best-of-3, all white 10mm, queue plus ballot plus debenture.

What surface is Wimbledon?

100% rye grass 8mm — low 1.0m bounce at 36 km/h, 3-shot rally vs 8 on clay.

How is final set decided now?

At 6-6 final set 10 by 2 since 2022 — Isner-Mahut 70-68 endless pre-2019 is gone.
