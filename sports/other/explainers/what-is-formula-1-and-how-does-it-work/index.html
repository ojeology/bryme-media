What is Formula 1 — and how does it work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Formula 1 is 20 drivers in 10 teams in single-seaters at 220 mph over 24 Grands Prix per season from Bahrain in March to Abu Dhabi in December, with points 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 to 10th per race, the constructors' sum of both drivers, and DRS and budget cap as the modern bottlenecks.

Explained properly

Formula 1 is 10 teams of 2 drivers — Red Bull, Mercedes, Ferrari, McLaren, Aston Martin, Alpine, Williams, RB, Sauber, Haas — each builds a 798kg hybrid single-seater with a 1.6L V6 turbo plus 120kW hybrid, at 220 mph on the straight, 5g in corners, with 18-inch Pirelli tyres in 5 compounds from soft to hard and intermediate and wet for rain, so the car, not just the driver, decides about 70% of pace by data.

Weekend explains the evergreen. Three practices Friday-Saturday, qualifying Saturday over Q1, Q2, Q3 knockout — 20, then 15, then 10 — with the fastest lap on soft taking pole, then the Grand Prix Sunday over 190 miles or 2 hours, 44-78 laps by track, with a formation lap, a standing start with 5 lights, and a pit window where the 2-second tyre change can undercut — the 24 weekends are the 24 tracks, from Monaco's 2-mile street to Spa's 4.3-mile mountain.

Points are 25 to 10th. Winner 25, 2nd 18, 3rd 15, 4th 12, 5th 10, 6th 8, 7th 6, 8th 4, 9th 2, 10th 1 plus fastest lap 1 if top 10, so a 1-2 team takes 43 and the 10th takes 1, and the constructors' championship is the sum of both drivers — Red Bull's 1st and 2nd in 2023 was 43 every Sunday, which is why the team title often follows the drivers' title.

DRS explains the overtake. The Drag Reduction System opens the rear wing on a straight if within 1 second of the car ahead at the detection point, adding about 15 mph and 20m of closing — the DRS zone is 600-800m, so the following car gains, not the leader, which is why the lead car defends by braking early to be within 1 second for the next DRS, and why Monaco has no DRS pass while Monza has 3 DRS zones.

The budget cap is the evergreen since 2021. Teams are capped at $135m per season for car development, not driver salaries, so wind-tunnel time is also capped by championship position — last gets 15% more wind tunnel than first — which is why the 10th in December gets more aero hours next year, not more money, and why the 2021-22 caps decided the 2023 pecking order more than the drivers did.

Picture this: Monza: 53 laps, 190 miles, Verstappen pole at 1:20.1 on soft, race Sunday with one stop soft to hard at lap 20, DRS on the 800m main straight adds 15 mph, Verstappen leads into Turn 1, Leclerc within 1 second gets DRS and closes 20m but cannot pass without over-cut, Red Bull 1-2 takes 43 points, Williams' 10th takes 1 — 24 Grands Prix, 25-1 points, DRS and cap decide more than the 220 mph top speed.

Myths vs reality

“Formula 1 is just fastest car wins.”
Reality: Car is about 70%, driver 30% — qualifying, DRS, strategy and the 2-second pit undercut decide the Sunday more than top speed.
“DRS is automatic.”
Reality: Only if within 1 second at the detection point — the lead car can brake to stay within 1 second for the next lap's DRS, so the zone is a chess, not a button.
“Teams spend unlimited.”
Reality: $135m budget cap plus wind-tunnel handicap — last gets 15% more aero hours than first, so December's 10th gets more development next year, not first.

FAQ

How many races in Formula 1?

24 in 2024 — Bahrain March to Abu Dhabi December, 20 drivers in 10 teams, 25-1 points to 10th per race, constructors sum of both drivers.

What is DRS?

Rear wing opens on a straight if within 1 second at detection, adding 15 mph and 20m — the overtake aid that decides Monza, not Monaco.

What is the budget cap?

$135m per team for car development plus wind-tunnel handicap — last gets 15% more aero than first, so the field converges.
