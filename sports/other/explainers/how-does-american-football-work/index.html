How does American football actually work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: American football is 11-a-side, 4 downs to gain 10 yards, 4x15 minutes (college 15, NFL 15) where the offence has 4 tries to move 10 yards or it hands over, 6 points for a touchdown in the end zone, 3 for a field goal through the posts, and the Super Bowl is the final between the AFC and NFC champions after the 14-team playoff.

Explained properly

The downs explain the game. Offence has 4 downs to advance 10 yards — 1st & 10 at the 30, then 2nd & 7 if 3 yards gained, 3rd & 2, 4th & 1 — gain 10 and the downs reset to 1st & 10, fail to gain 10 on 4th and the ball turns over on downs where it died. That is why 3rd down is the money down — about 40% conversion in the NFL, so 3rd & 5 is a pass, 3rd & 1 is a run, and 4th down is usually a punt to flip field, not a try.

Scoring is 6+1 or 3. Touchdown is 6 when the ball crosses the goal line into the 10-yard end zone — carry or catch with two feet in — then the extra point is 1 from a 33-yard kick or 2 from a 2-yard scrimmage, so 7 or 8, and a field goal is 3 through the 18.5-foot uprights from anywhere. A 20-yard field goal vs a 2-yard touchdown is the 3 vs 7 decision that decides games, which is why coaches go for it on 4th & 2 at the 5-yard line — 7 beats 3.

Time is 4x15 in college and NFL with a stop-start clock — the clock stops on incomplete passes, out of bounds, scores, and inside 2 minutes the two-minute warning — so a 15-minute quarter takes 40 minutes real and the 60-minute game takes 3 hours with ads, which is why the final 2 minutes can have 7 stoppages and a 35-31 can become 38-35 on the last play.

The playoff explains the Super Bowl. The NFL is 32 teams in 8 divisions, 14 make the playoffs — 7 per conference AFC and NFC — seeded 1-7, then single elimination divisional, championship and Super Bowl at a neutral — the Super Bowl is not a series, it is one game in February where the conference champions meet, with a halftime show that is longer than the quarter break.

Why padding is the tackle. Helmets, shoulder pads and the line of scrimmage make American football a collision sport — the hitting is head-up but the tackling is law, with no targeting of the head and a flag for helmet-to-helmet, which is why the NFL's concussion protocol and the 2018 helmet rule changed the tackle as much as rugby's did, though the pads let the speed stay higher.

Picture this: Fourth & 1 at the 40 with 1:20 left: Kansas City can punt 40 yards to pin at the 10, or go for 1 yard to keep the ball and kneel. Mahomes sneaks 2 yards for 1st & 10, the downs reset, Kansas City kneels twice and the game ends 24-21 — the 4 downs to gain 10 are the 10-yard chain that decides punt vs go, not the scoreboard.

Myths vs reality

“American football is 4 tries to score.”
Reality: 4 downs to gain 10 yards, not to score — gain 10 and the downs reset, fail and the ball turns over where it died.
“A touchdown is 7 points.”
Reality: 6 plus the extra point 1 or 2-point conversion — 6 alone, 7 with the kick, 8 with the 2-point — so 7 is 6+1, not automatic.
“The Super Bowl is a series.”
Reality: One game in February between the AFC and NFC champions after a 14-team single-elimination playoff — no series.

FAQ

How many downs to gain 10 yards?

4 — 1st & 10, then 2nd, 3rd, 4th. Gain 10 and the downs reset to 1st & 10, fail on 4th and the ball turns over.

How long is an American football game?

60 minutes — 4x15 — but about 3 hours real with stoppages, incomplete passes, timeouts and ads, especially the last 2 minutes.

How many points is a touchdown?

6, plus 1 for the kick or 2 for the conversion — so 7 or 8, not 7 alone, plus 3 for a field goal.
