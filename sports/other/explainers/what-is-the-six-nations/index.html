What is the Six Nations?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The Six Nations is rugby union's 6-team, 5-round league each February-March — England, France, Ireland, Wales, Scotland and Italy play each other once, 2 home 3 away alternating yearly, 4 points for a win, bonus for 4 tries or losing by 7, with the Grand Slam for 5 wins and the Wooden Spoon for 0.

Explained properly

The Six Nations is 6 teams in a single round-robin — 5 games per nation over 7 weeks in February-March, with one bye week and one fallow week, so the championship has 15 games total, each 80 minutes, with no semi or final — the table after 5 decides, which is why the last Saturday has three games at 14:15, 16:45 and 20:00 to stagger the deciders, like football's last day but with 5, not 38.

Points are 4 for a win, 2 for a draw, 1 bonus for 4 tries, 1 bonus for losing by 7 or less — so a team can get 2 points from a loss with 4 tries and a close margin, and a 5-0 Grand Slam winner gets a 3-point bonus for the Slam, so 5 wins is 20 plus 3 = 23 max, while a 4-1 without bonuses is 16 — the bonus makes a 4-try win more valuable than a 3-try win even with the same 4 points for the win.

The venues alternate yearly. England at 82,000 Twickenham with 82,000 for France, France at 80,000 Stade de France, Ireland at 51,700 Aviva, Wales at 73,000 Principality, Scotland at 67,000 Murrayfield, Italy at 68,000 Olimpico — the 2-home-3-away flip each year decides the title as much as form, because a year with England away at France and Ireland is harder than the home year.

Why the Six Nations is not the Rugby Championship is the hemisphere. The Rugby Championship is 4 teams — New Zealand, South Africa, Australia, Argentina — in July-September with 2x home-and-away, so the Six Nations' February cold and rain produces forward-led low-scoring games, while the Championship's July sun produces running rugby — the same sport in opposite seasons, which is why the Six Nations winner and the Rugby Championship winner meet in the autumn internationals to decide the world number one.

The Wooden Spoon is the evergreen myth. No trophy, but the last-placed team is said to hold the Spoon — Italy have 18 since joining in 2000, Wales have 12, England 12 historically — and the Spoon in 2023 was Italy who beat Wales in Cardiff to avoid it, so the bottom game in round 5 can be as charged as the top, because the Spoon, like relegation, is the anti-title that still fills the stadium.

Picture this: Last Saturday 2023: Ireland need a win at home vs England to win the Slam — 4 points plus 3-point Slam bonus, France need a bonus-point win and an Ireland loss to overtake, Wales vs Italy is Spoon decider in Rome — Ireland win 29-10 with 4 tries for the Slam and 13-point bonus, France win with bonus but stay on 19, so the Slam bonus decides the table by 3, not points difference alone.

Myths vs reality

“The Six Nations is knockout.”
Reality: Single round-robin 5 games per nation over 7 weeks, 4 points for a win plus try and losing bonuses, Grand Slam bonus for 5 wins, no semi or final.
“Bonus points decide draws.”
Reality: 4 tries gets 1 bonus win or lose, losing by 7 gets 1 — a 4-try loss beats a 3-try win on bonuses, which is why teams chase the 4th try at 30-10 even when the win is gone.
“It is always the same venues.”
Reality: 2 home 3 away alternating yearly — a year with France and Ireland away is harder than the home year, which decides many titles.

FAQ

How many games in the Six Nations?

15 — 6 teams, 5 per nation over 7 weeks in Feb-March, 4 points per win plus 4-try and losing bonuses, 3-point Grand Slam bonus.

What is the Grand Slam?

5 wins from 5 — 20 points plus 3-point Slam bonus for 23 max, the only unbeaten path in the Six Nations.

What is the Wooden Spoon?

The last-placed team is said to hold the Spoon — no trophy, but the bottom game in round 5 is the anti-title that still sells out.
