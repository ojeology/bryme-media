What is Duckworth-Lewis in cricket?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: Duckworth-Lewis-Stern is the rain rule that resets the target by overs and wickets lost, not just overs — the resource is overs x wickets, so 50 overs with 10 wickets is 100% resource, 20 overs with 8 left is 68%, and the target is par plus one, used in 2023 World Cup 4 games, not just a run-rate.

Explained properly

DLS is resource, not rate. overs x wickets is resource — 50 overs 10 wickets 100%, 20 overs 10 wickets 52% by table, 20 overs with 8 left 48% — the table's resource % decides the par score for the chaser, so a chase of 300 in 50 needs 60 per 10 not 6 per over flat — the wickets in hand add resource, which is why 100-2 in 20 needs more than 100-5 in 20, not the same as run-rate's 5 per over.

The calculation steps explain the myth. Team 1 300 in 50 overs 100% — rain cuts chase to 30 overs for Team 2, Team 2 resource 59% (30 overs 10 wickets) — target = 300 * 59/100 = 177 plus 1 = 178 to win if no wickets lost at cut — if Team 2 was 100-2 in 20 at cut, resource used 32% plus remaining 18 overs 4 wickets 42% = 74% resource, so target = 300*74/100=222, not 178 — the wickets before cut shift the par that run-rate would miss by 44 runs.

The Stern revision added modern scoring. Since 2014 the DLS includes 200+ scores in ODIs, so the par for 350 is not 300*0.59 but 350's resource adjusted for high-scoring, which is why the 2023 World Cup's 4 rain games at DLS had targets 340 par plus 1, not 300 — the Stern raised high-score DLS by 3% vs high-rate, which is why the par 150 in T20 under DLS is not 250*0.5 but 250's table at 20 overs 57%.

The evergreen bottleneck is 20 overs needed. DLS needs 20 overs minimum in ODI for a result, 5 in T20 — under 20 overs ballsed, no result if rain before 20, so the 18-over chase that rain stops at 18-3 is no result, not DLS at 18 — the 20 is the threshold that 2019 and 2023 World Cup both have as the DLS cut line, which is why the TV shows DLS par plus 1 only after 20.

The T20 DLS is similar with 20 as 100% resource — 10 overs 10 wickets is 63% resource, not 50%, so 180 chase in 10 overs is 180*0.63/100 = 114 plus 1 =115, not 90 — the overs x wickets resource table's curve is concave, not linear, which is why 10 overs needs 63% not 50% — the curve is the table's heart that run-rate's linear 9 per over misses.

Picture this: 2023 World Cup rain: India 274-6 in 50, Pakistan chase rain at 100-2 in 20, DLS par 140 to win, target 222 with 30 remaining with 8 wickets — no result before 20 overs, 20 is minimum for DLS — the overs x wickets resource table not run-rate, now Stern with high-score 3% lift, 50 100% vs 20 52% vs 20 with 8 wickets 48%.

Myths vs reality

“Rain means run-rate.”
Reality: DLS resource overs x wickets — 50 100% vs 20 with 10 wickets 52% vs 20 with 8 left 48% — wickets matter, not just overs.
“Target is pro-rata.”
Reality: Target = Team1 * resource for 30 overs 59% — 300 needs 178 in 30, but 100-2 in 20 needs 222 not 178 — wickets before cut shift.
“Any overs counts.”
Reality: Minimum 20 overs in ODI, 5 in T20 for DLS — 18-over chase rain before 20 is no result, not DLS.

FAQ

What does DLS do?

Resets target by overs x wickets resource — 50 overs 10 wickets 100% vs 30 overs 59%, not just run-rate's overs linear.

What is minimum overs for DLS?

20 in ODI, 5 in T20 — no result if rain before 20 in ODI, not DLS at 18.

Why DLS not run-rate?

Wickets matter — 100-2 in 20 needs 222 not 178, because 100-2 resource is 74% vs 59% for 0-0 in 30, so wickets shift par 44 runs.
