How do Formula 1 tyres work?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: F1 tyres are 5 dry compounds C1 hard to C5 soft plus intermediate and wet, with 2 dry mandatory per Grand Prix, pit window 1-2 stops, degradation 0.1s per lap vs 0.5s cliff, and the undercut vs overcut that 1 second pit vs 23s pit lane decides.

Explained properly

F1 tyres are 18-inch Pirelli — 5 dry C1 hard (white) to C5 soft (red) plus C2-4 yellow, plus intermediate green and wet blue — each GP picks 3 dry of the 5: e.g. Bahrain C1-C2-C3, Monaco C3-C4-C5 — the 3 per GP plus 8 sets dry per weekend (soft 3 sets, medium 2, hard 1, plus sprint 2 extra?) — the tyre life is 25 laps soft, 35 medium, 45 hard on 2024's abrasiveness, so the degradation cliff at lap 12 for soft's 0.5s per lap after cliff vs 0.1s linear before decides the pit lap, not the fuel, which is why soft starter pits lap 18 while hard starter lap 30.

The 2-dry rule decides strategy. The Grand Prix must use 2 of the 3 dry compounds, so a soft start must finish medium or hard plus mandatory 1 pit — the 1 pit 23 seconds pit lane vs 0.1s per lap degradation makes the undercut vs overcut: pit lap 16 to undercut the leader's pit 17 with fresh medium 1 sec per lap faster — the undercut's 2s gap closes, which is why Verstappen at Monaco pits 16 from soft to hard to undercut Leclerc's 18, not stay out for overcut 20 with clear air but 0.5s degradation cliff.

The evergreen myth is soft always fastest. Soft is 0.8s per lap faster lap 1-5 but degrades 0.5s after cliff lap 12 vs medium's 0.2s after lap 25, so soft at lap 20 is slower than medium new — the soft's 25-lap life vs medium's 35 shows the crossover lap 18 where medium new beats soft old by 1.2s, which is why the 2-stop soft-medium-soft vs 1-stop medium-hard decides Monaco's 1-pit not 2 as tyre choice's crossover.

The pit lane 23 seconds plus stationary 2.1s vs 3.5s slow shows stop's window — the 23s at 60 km/h pit lane vs 350 km/h straight's gap 23s pit lane loses 23s plus 2.1s stop =25s total, so the undercut needs 0.8s per lap x 3 laps =2.4s to recover, which is why undercut lap 16 with 3-lap fresh delta recovers 2.4s vs leader's old soft degradation, not the 0.5 cliff alone — the 23s is the evergreen that makes 2-stop not 1 if tyre degradation's 0.5s after cliff times 10 laps =5s vs pit 25s, so 2-stop loses 50s vs 1-stop 25s but gains 1.5s per lap x 20 =30s, net 20s loss.

The sprint's one-tyre myth vs GP's 2. Sprint 100km uses one tyre no mandatory, GP 305km uses 2 mandatory, so sprint's soft 15 laps 30 minutes with no pit wins 0.5s per lap vs GP's soft-medium must 2 compounds — the 100km sprint's one tyre shows why sprint at Interlagos 24 laps soft only works but GP 71 laps not — soft life 25 not 71, so GP needs medium-hard 45.

Picture this: Bahrain GP: 57 laps — 3 compounds soft C3 medium C2 hard C1 — Verstappen soft 18 laps then hard 39 laps 1-stop undercut Leclerc lap 17 medium, pit 23s +2.1s stop — soft 0.8 faster laps1-5 but medium new crossover lap18 beats soft old by 1.2s, so medium after cliff's 0.1 vs 0.5 decides — the 2-dry rule's soft-medium vs sprint's one-tyre no pit 100km with one soft not two.

Myths vs reality

“Soft is always fastest.”
Reality: Soft 0.8s faster laps1-5 but 0.5 cliff after 12 vs medium 0.1 — soft old at lap 20 slower than medium new crossover at 18 by 1.2s.
“One tyre per race.”
Reality: Grand Prix must use 2 of 3 dry — sprint 100km no mandatory, GP 305km 1-2 stops mandatory, so soft only not allowed at GP.
“Pit is 2 seconds.”
Reality: Stationary 2.1s plus pit lane 23s =25s — the undercut's 0.8×3=2.4s needs 3 laps fresh to recover 25s.

FAQ

How many tyre compounds in F1?

5 dry C1-C5 plus intermediate and wet — 3 per GP chosen (e.g. C1-3 Bahrain), 2 mandatory at GP.

What is undercut?

Pit 1 lap early with fresh tyre to pass — 0.8s per lap fresh recovers 25s pit lane in 3 laps.

Does sprint need 2 compounds?

No, sprint 100km one tyre no pit, GP 305km 2 compounds mandatory — soft only illegal at GP.
