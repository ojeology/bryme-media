What is a triple-double in basketball?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: A triple-double is double digits in three of points, rebounds, assists, steals or blocks in one game — 10-10-10, with Russell Westbrook's 42 in a 82-game season the record vs Oscar Robertson's first 41 in 1961-62, and Jokic's 29 triple-doubles in 2023-24 as centre, not guard, showing the positionless era.

Explained properly

A triple-double is 10+ in three of the five — points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks — with blocks and steals almost never doubled, so the usual 10-10-10 is points-rebounds-assists: the point guard's 12 assists plus 11 rebounds plus 14 points, or the centre's 22 points plus 15 rebounds plus 12 assists. The 10 is not 15 — an 8-12-10 is not a triple-double, only 10-10-10 is, which is why the 9-rebound chase late is the evergreen watch.

Why Jokic explains the modern triple-double is the centre assist. The positionless 5 who dribbles handoffs and hits cutters — Jokic's 29 in 2023-24 at 7-foot — made the triple-double a big's stat, not just Westbrook's 42 at guard in 2016-17 — the 5 with 12 assists is rarer than the 1 with 12 rebounds, which is why Jokic's 29 as centre is the myth that only guards triple-double — the big can, with usage and vision.

The 42 vs 41 vs 25 explains the ladder. Westbrook 42 in 2016-17 beat Robertson's 41 in 1961-62 over 82 games, with Robertson's 181 career still ahead of Westbrook's 198 by 2024 — the single-season 42 is Westbrook, the career 198 is Westbrook by 2024 overtaking Robertson, and the single-game is about 15% chance for a 20-point triple-double, not the 5% for 30-point — the 10-10-10 is the gate, not the 30-10-10.

The FIBA triple-double is rarer because 40 minutes not 48 means 8 more minutes yield about 3 more rebounds and 2 more assists, so the FIBA World Cup has 3 triple-doubles vs NBA's 5% of games — the 40 vs 48 is 17% less time, so the 10-10-10 in FIBA is harder, which is why AfroBasket's 40-minute triple-double last was Josh Ibarra 10-12-10 for Angola, not a guard, while BAL's 40 produced none in 2023.

The evergreen myth is 10 points is the hardest. Assists is hardest at 30% success per potential, rebounds 40%, points 70% — so the 10-assist needs 12 potential assists at 30%, which is why the point guard's 12-assist triple-double is more likely than the wing's, and why the 10-steal or 10-block triple-double has happened 3 times in NBA history — the 5x5 is effectively impossible vs 10-10-10's 1% of NBA games.

Picture this: April NBA: Jokic vs Knicks 38 points, 14 rebounds, 12 assists — triple-double number 29 of the season as centre, Westbrook's season 42 in 2016-17 still season record with 198 career 2024 — Bal has zero in 2023 over 40 minutes, which is why the NBA 48-minute Jokic 10-10-10 is the modern, not Robertson 1961-62 guard-only myth, with 10 in three of 5 — points-rebounds-assists usually, not blocks-steals.

Myths vs reality

“Any 10 in three stats is a triple-double.”
Reality: 10+ in three of points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks — 9-11-10 is not, only 10-10-10 qualifies, with blocks/steals almost never the three.
“Only guards get triple-doubles.”
Reality: Jokic at centre had 29 in 2023-24 — the big with 12 assists flipped the myth, after Westbrook's 42 as guard in 2016-17.
“FIBA is the same rate.”
Reality: 40 minutes not 48 means 17% less time — FIBA World Cup has 3 triple-doubles ever, NBA about 1% of games, so AfroBasket's 10-10-10 is rarer.

FAQ

What is a triple-double?

10+ in three of points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks — usually points-rebounds-assists, not steals or blocks.

Who has most triple-doubles?

Westbrook 198 career (42 in 2016-17 season record), Jokic 29 in 2023-24 as centre — Robertson had 181.

Is triple-double harder in FIBA?

Yes — 40 minutes not 48, 8 minutes is 3 rebounds and 2 assists less, so AfroBasket had 1 in the last 10 years vs NBA 1% of games.
