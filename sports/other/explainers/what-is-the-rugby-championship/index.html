What is the Rugby Championship?

Evergreen • Updated 02 Oct 2026 • 3 min read
Answer: The Rugby Championship is the southern 4-nation July-September — New Zealand, South Africa, Australia and Argentina play home-and-away double round-robin 6 games each over 7 weeks, 12 games total, 4 points for win plus try and losing bonuses, not the Six Nations' single round where February cold not July sun decides.

Explained properly

The Rugby Championship is 4 nations 6 games each — each plays the other 3 twice, home and away, 2 vs NZ at Eden Park, 2 vs SA at Ellis Park, 2 vs ARG at Vélez — 12 games total over 7 weeks with one bye per team — the Championship's 6 per team vs Six Nations' 5 per team is one more game, but the Championship's 2x home-and-away vs Six Nations' single 2H-3A alternate yearly — the 2x means the Ellis Park altitude 1,750m's advantage repeats for SA not just alternate, which is why NZ's Eden Park 30-year unbeaten home vs Six Nations' Twickenham 82k alternate matters differently.

Points as Six Nations: 4 for win plus bonus for 4 tries or losing by 7, so 6-game max 30 plus? Actually 6 wins =24 plus 6 try bonuses maybe 30, not 23 as Six Nations' 5-0 Slam bonus 3 — the Championship's 6-0 not 5-0 has no Slam bonus 3, so the 6-0 max is 24 plus 6 =30 with no extra, which is why NZ's 6-0 2023 with 5 try bonuses 29 beats SA 4-2 with 2 bonuses 18 by 11 not 3 as Slam would.

The evergreen is Argentina 2012. Argentina joined 2012 as 4th after Tri Nations 1996-2011 with 3 — the 4th made 6 from 4, not 4 from 3 — the Pumas' 2022 win over NZ at Christchurch and 2023 over Australia at Parra shows the 4th not dead weight — Wales' Six Nations Wooden Spoon 2023 with no wins vs Argentina's 2-4 still competitive, which is why the Championship's 4 closed not 6 as Six Nations with Italy, so Arg never Spoon but can be 4th with 0-6.

The myth is closed 4 forever. The Championship's closed 4 vs Six Nations' fixed 6 — no promotion as Europe's Rugby Championship plus Georgia vs 2023 6-0 Portugal repechage not — the Championship's 4 plus Japan invite talk for 5 has no promotion, like EuroLeague's 18 licences not open qualifier, so the Championship's 12 games vs Six Nations' 15 with similar TV dollars shows the 4's double round has 12 vs 15 with not open promotion.

The July-September sun vs February rain explains style. The Championship's July winter in NZ/SA is 10°C dry with hard pitches vs Six Nations February 5°C wet heavy — the hard not heavy favours running rugby with 6 tries per game vs forward wet with 3, which is why the final's 6-game double round's running shows where Six Nations' 5-game forward shows the autumn internations November cross-hemisphere to decide world 1, not just the hemisphere's table.

Picture this: 2023 Championship: 4 nations 6 each — NZ 5-1 24 points, SA 4-2 18, Argentina 2-4 11, Australia 1-5 7 — 12 games over 7 weeks July-September 4 points + bonus, Argentina beat Australia 34-31 at Parra — the southern double round 6 vs northern single 5, July sun 10°C hard vs February rain 5°C wet, 4 closed with no promotion as Six Nations 6.

Myths vs reality

“Rugby Championship is Six Nations southern.”
Reality: 4 nations 6 games each double round over 7 weeks July-September — NZ, SA, Australia, Argentina 12 games total, not 6 single 5.
“It is Six Nations in south.”
Reality: 6 per team vs 5 — no Slam bonus 3, so 6-0 max 30 with 6 bonuses not 23 — double home-and-away, not single 2H-3A alternate.
“Argentina never wins.”
Reality: Beat NZ 2022 at Christchurch and Australia 2023 — 2-4 not 0-6 Wooden Spoon as Wales in Six Nations.
