Who Is Leading the 2026 Ballon d'Or Race? Our Picks for the Players Who Could Win

This is our assessment, not an official Ballon d'Or ranking. Only one player can win. Based on the 2025/26 club season and the World Cup, Harry Kane is our leading pick, with Lamine Yamal and Rodri the strongest challenges.

Ibrahim Sodiq2026-08-1911 min read

A race, not a result

The 2026 Ballon d'Or ceremony is in London on 26 October. UEFA confirmed the date and the city in May. It is the 70th edition. Ousmane Dembélé and Aitana Bonmatí still hold last year's awards. France Football has not published this year's 30-name list. The winner has not been named.

That does not mean the season left nothing to judge. The 2025/26 club campaign is finished. The World Cup is finished. Spain are world champions. Paris Saint-Germain retained the Champions League. Harry Kane won the European Golden Shoe. Voters will have to weigh those things against each other. Only one player can win the men's award. The rest of this page is our current prediction of who has the strongest case — not a UEFA ranking, not a France Football shortlist, and not a claim about who will lift the trophy in October.

If France Football changes the voting method, their announcement is the source. Last year, one specialised journalist from each of the top 100 FIFA-ranked nations picked ten names from a list of 30. Those ten received 15, 12, 10, 8, 7, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 point. That is how 2025 was decided. It is the method we are writing against, unless they say otherwise.

In our view, the strongest cases currently belong to five players. They are not equally likely. We ranked them.

Our current top 5

1. Harry Kane (England, Bayern Munich) — our No. 1. 2. Lamine Yamal (Spain, Barcelona) — the biggest threat to Kane. 3. Rodri (Spain, Manchester City) — the World Cup argument in one player. 4. Kylian Mbappé (France, Real Madrid) — the scoring case without the biggest trophies. 5. Lionel Messi (Argentina, Inter Miami) — still elite, and a finalist, not a champion.

Below is why we have them in that order, what each man actually did, and what would have to change for the ranking to move.

1. Harry Kane — our No. 1

Kane's 2025/26 season at Bayern is the cleanest individual scoring year in Europe. FC Bayern's own announcement put him on 36 Bundesliga goals and 72 Golden Shoe points, clear of Erling Haaland (27 league goals, 54 points) and Kylian Mbappé (25, 50). He scored 61 times in 51 appearances in all competitions. After the domestic double — league and DFB-Pokal — he called it the best season of his career. Bayern said he scored a hat-trick in the cup final.

The Golden Shoe is his second. The first was 2023/24, also with 36 league goals. That is not a purple patch. It is a standard.

In Europe he was one goal off the Champions League scoring title. UEFA's official list has Mbappé on 15 in 11 matches and Kane on 14. Kane scored in the semi-final second leg against Paris. Bayern did not reach the final. Paris retained the trophy against Arsenal on penalties in Budapest. Kane's club year is a German double and a European near-miss, not a treble.

For England he reached a World Cup semi-final and lost it. Argentina came from behind in Atlanta: Anthony Gordon scored, Enzo Fernández and Lautaro Martínez answered late, 2-1. FIFA's Golden Boot table has Kane on six World Cup goals. Useful. Not decisive. England did not play in the final.

His strongest Ballon d'Or argument is the combination voters rarely get in the same player: unmatched league scoring, a second Golden Shoe, and actual club trophies. Numbers without silverware lose. Silverware without numbers looks like a supporting act. Kane has both.

His biggest weakness is the ceiling above the Bundesliga. He did not win the Champions League. He did not win the World Cup. A voter who treats those two competitions as the only stages that matter can leave him off the top of the ballot and still sleep.

Why he could win: because the season's scoring table is not close, and Bayern won the two domestic cups that were in front of them. Why he might not: because Yamal and Rodri can put a World Cup winner's medal on the table, and Kane cannot.

2. Lamine Yamal — our No. 2

Yamal's case is built on more than goals, which is the point. Barcelona's own season recap made him LaLiga player of the year after a title-winning campaign: 16 league goals, 11 assists, and no other player in the division provided as many passes that became goals. He shared the Zarra trophy with Ferran Torres. It was his highest-scoring league season. He is still a teenager.

Then he won the World Cup. Spain beat Argentina 1-0 after extra time in New Jersey. Ferran Torres scored in the 106th minute. FIFA recorded it. Barcelona called Yamal the youngest player ever to win both the European Championship and the World Cup. That sentence will follow him into every ballot conversation in October.

If voters place enormous weight on international success and on the player who makes a champion side more dangerous, Yamal can overtake Kane. Last year he finished second in the official men's vote, behind Dembélé, with 1,059 points. He already knows what a Ballon d'Or night looks like from the row below the winner.

His strongest argument is influence plus the biggest prize in the sport. A winger who creates as much as he finishes, in a league-winning side, then lifts the World Cup before he is 20, is not a sentimental pick. It is a coherent one.

His biggest weakness is the raw scoring gap. Kane's 36 league goals and 61 in all competitions are a different sport from 16 and 11. Yamal did not win the Golden Shoe. Barcelona did not win the Champions League — Paris did. A voter who wants a centre-forward's numbers will not be argued out of Kane by a highlight reel.

Why he could win: because the World Cup still moves this award, and Yamal was not a passenger in Spain's. Why he might not: because Kane's season is easier to put on a spreadsheet, and some ballots still look like spreadsheets.

3. Rodri — our No. 3

Rodri's case is a different sport again. FIFA gave him the adidas Golden Ball as the outstanding player of the 2026 World Cup. Spain won all the way through, conceded one goal in the tournament — Belgium's Charles De Ketelaere — and beat Argentina in extra time. FIFA's own write-up of the champions put Rodri at the centre of that control: when he raised the tempo, Spain raised it; when he broke the other side's rhythm, the team stayed in shape.

That is not a goals-and-assists argument. It is the argument that the most important player in the world champions is the most important player of the year. Rodri already won this award in 2024. He knows the room.

His strongest Ballon d'Or case is simple to say and hard to measure: he was the midfielder the World Cup was built around, and FIFA's technical group agreed. You cannot watch Spain's tournament and pretend the No. 16 was furniture.

His biggest weakness is the same fact, flipped. Forwards leave numbers that travel across languages. Rodri leaves control. Last year's official top ten was almost entirely attackers and creators. A midfielder has to be obviously the best player at the biggest tournament to break that habit. He was. Whether 100 journalists, filling in ten names in a hotel the week the shortlist drops, will all see it the same way is another question.

We do not have a clean official account of his club scoring that belongs on this page, and we are not going to invent one. His Ballon d'Or case in 2026 runs through Spain, not through a Golden Shoe table.

Why he could win: because the Golden Ball at a World Cup is the loudest individual honour the sport hands out between Ballon d'Or nights. Why he might not: because Kane and Yamal give voters a simpler story, and simple stories get 15 points.

4. Kylian Mbappé — our No. 4

Mbappé has one of the strongest individual scoring years of anyone in this race. Real Madrid and UEFA both published the same Champions League line: 15 goals in 11 matches, top scorer, including 13 in the league phase — a record for that stage — and a goal in each quarter-final leg against Bayern. Only Cristiano Ronaldo has scored more in a single Champions League season. Bayern still knocked Madrid out.

In the league he finished third on the Golden Shoe list with 25 goals. That is elite. It is also 11 behind Kane.

At the World Cup he won the adidas Golden Boot. FIFA's official scoring table has him on 10 goals and four assists. He also took the Bronze Ball. France did not take the trophy. They lost to Spain in the semi-final and then lost the third-place match to England. FIFA's own recap put France fourth.

His strongest argument is volume in the two competitions that travel: the Champions League scoring title and the World Cup Golden Boot in the same year. Very few players ever hold both conversations at once.

His biggest weakness is the trophy cabinet for 2025/26. Real Madrid did not win the Champions League. France did not win the World Cup. Kane has a domestic double. Yamal and Rodri have a world title. Mbappé has individual prizes and a fourth-place medal. That is not nothing. It is not the same as winning the thing the year is named after.

Why he could win: because some ballots still follow the top scorer, and he was the top scorer in Europe's biggest club competition and at the World Cup. Why he might not: because the players above him can point to a league title, a cup, or a World Cup, and he cannot.

5. Lionel Messi — our No. 5

Treat this case with the respect the year earned. Messi played in a sixth World Cup. Inter Miami's official tournament recap has him in all eight of Argentina's matches, with eight goals and four assists, through to the final. FIFA's Golden Boot table matches those eight goals. He took the Silver Ball, behind Rodri, and the Silver Boot, behind Mbappé. Argentina were the defending champions. They left New Jersey as runners-up.

The individual production is still ridiculous at his age. A hat-trick in the opener against Algeria. The all-time World Cup scoring record, broken during the group stage. Two assists in the semi-final comeback against England. Then a final he did not win. Spain's extra-time goal ended the defence of the trophy.

His strongest argument is that he remained the attacking reference point of a team that reached the last night of the World Cup, and that the numbers still look like a prime. Creativity did not dry up because the calendar said 2026.

His biggest weakness is the result that night, and the league he plays in. Reaching a World Cup final without winning it is a magnificent year and a harder Ballon d'Or year. The players above him either scored more in Europe, won their domestic league, or won the tournament. We are not going to invent an MLS scoring line to pad this section. His 2026 case is the World Cup. The World Cup ended 1-0 to Spain.

Why he could win: because a generation of journalists has watched him decide nights like this before, and eight goals to a final is not a farewell tour. Why he might not: because the award, in our view, should not go to the second-best team at the World Cup when the first-best team has Yamal and Rodri on the same ballot, and when Kane's club season is sitting there in plain sight.

Who has the strongest case?

The live argument is Kane against Yamal against Rodri. Mbappé and Messi are in the race. They are not, in our current ranking, about to win it unless the three above them all lose the room.

Kane against Yamal is volume against occasion. Kane has the Golden Shoe, the 61-goal club year, and a domestic double. Yamal has a league title, the league's player-of-the-season award, and a World Cup. If you think the Ballon d'Or is a season MVP for club football in Europe, Kane is ahead. If you think it is a prize for the footballer who most changed the biggest nights, Yamal is close enough to steal it.

Rodri against both of them is a category error on purpose. He will not beat Kane on goals. He will not beat Yamal on spectacle. He can beat both if enough voters decide that Spain's tournament was the event of the year and that he was the reason it looked inevitable. FIFA already made that call with the Golden Ball. The Ballon d'Or jury is larger, and more attached to forwards.

That is why Kane currently leads our editorial ranking. His year is the one you can defend in a single sentence without hedging: best scorer in Europe, champion in Germany, twice. Yamal's sentence needs a second clause about the World Cup. Rodri's sentence needs the listener to care about midfield. We care. We still have Kane first.

The picture can change. An official 30-name list from France Football would tell us who is actually on the ballot. A change in the voting method would tell us how those names are scored. Neither has been published as of 19 August 2026. Until it is, this is our view of the evidence in front of us — not theirs.

Final verdict

Our current pick: Harry Kane.

Only one player can win. Based on the club season that has actually been completed, the trophies that were actually lifted, and the World Cup that has already been played, Kane has the strongest overall case. Yamal is the player most likely to take it off him, because Spain won the tournament that will still be in voters' heads in October. Rodri is the player who makes the ranking honest: if the award is for the most important footballer of the year, not the most prolific, he is in the conversation whether the table likes it or not.

This is our assessment, not an official Ballon d'Or ranking. The ceremony is in London on 26 October. Come back when France Football names the 30, or when someone lifts the thing. Until then, Kane is where we would put the 15 points.
