Football history

Why was the away goals rule scrapped?

The short answer

UEFA abolished the away goals rule from the 2021-22 season because it had stopped doing its job: instead of encouraging away teams to attack, it increasingly pushed home teams in the second leg to play cautiously, and the statistical edge it once created had faded.

The rule, used in European competitions from 1965, said that if a two-legged tie finished level on aggregate, the team that scored more goals away from home advanced. It was introduced when long away trips were genuinely hostile and scoring on the road was rare, so an away goal was treated as worth more than one scored at home.

Over the decades, travel improved and away records across European football got better. Analysis UEFA published when scrapping the rule found that home advantage had declined, and that the rule was distorting behaviour: the team playing at home in the second leg knew a single away goal against them could effectively decide the tie, so they defended more, not less.

The replacement is straightforward: level ties now go to extra time and penalties at the end of the second leg. A few competitions outside UEFA still used away goals after 2021, so when you read an older match report the phrase tells you which era the tie belongs to — and the tiebreak's story is really the story of how much easier away travel became.

In practice

The classic away-goals scenario: first leg finishes 1-0 to the home side, and in the second leg the visitors equalise the tie. Under the old rule, that away goal meant the hosts needed two more; under the current rules, the tie simply goes to extra time. The scoreboard reads the same, but the incentives on the pitch are completely different.

Common misunderstandings

  • "Away goals still applies in the Champions League." It was removed from all UEFA club competitions from 2021-22. Level ties go to extra time and penalties.
  • "The rule was scrapped because it was unfair to big clubs." The official reasoning was about behaviour and declining home advantage — the rule was reshaping how teams attacked rather than rewarding ambition.

Quick questions

Is the away goals rule used anywhere today?

A small number of competitions kept it for a while — mostly outside UEFA — but the major European competitions no longer use it in any round.

What replaced it?

Extra time and a penalty shootout at the end of the second leg if the aggregate score is level after 180 minutes.
