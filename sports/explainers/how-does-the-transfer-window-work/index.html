Transfers & money

How does the transfer window work?

The short answer

A transfer window is a registration period. Clubs can only register new players during the two windows most leagues run each year — a long summer window and a short mid-season one in January — even though deals can be negotiated at any time.

The key word is registration, not negotiation. Two clubs can agree a deal in March; the player still cannot play for his new team until the next window opens and the registration is processed. That is why January is full of pre-agreements for summer moves, and why 'he signed in March' almost always means 'the move was agreed in March and completed in June'.

The two windows are not identical everywhere. England's summer window typically runs from early June to the start of September, with a January window across the turn of the year. Other countries shift by a few weeks, which occasionally lets a player leave one league after its deadline and still be registered in another. FIFA sets the framework; each association confirms its own dates.

There are genuine exceptions to 'no window, no move'. Unattached free agents can be registered outside a window. Leagues also allow limited emergency arrangements — goalkeeper loans in some competitions, for example — and youth registrations follow their own rules. Cup competitions add a second layer: a new signing is often cup-tied or simply not registered in time for the next round.

In practice

A club signs a defender on 30 January. The league registration goes through, so he can play domestically — but the Champions League registration deadline passed days earlier, so he watches the knockout tie from the stands. Nothing about his transfer was late; only one of the competitions he joined has a closed list.

Common misunderstandings

  • "No transfers happen outside the window." Deals are negotiated year-round. Only the registration is windowed — which is why transfer news never stops.
  • "Every league's window opens and closes on the same day." Dates differ by country, and occasionally by a few days. That gap is how late-deadline moves between leagues stay possible.

Quick questions

Can a free agent sign after the window closes?

Yes. A player without a club can normally be registered outside the window, subject to league rules on when he can actually play.

What is deadline day?

The final day of a window, when most leagues process registrations until a published cut-off time. Deals are often agreed on the day and confirmed in the final hours.
