Athletics · Track sprints

How does a 100m race work?

The short answer

A 100m race is a straight sprint in which each athlete stays in a separate lane from start to finish. Sprinters use starting blocks, react to the gun and are placed when their torso reaches the finish-line plane. Wind is measured too: for World Athletics record ratification, a tailwind above 2.0 metres per second is too strong, although it does not erase the race result.

The 100 metres is run on the straight of a standard outdoor track. The staggered lane positions used in a 200m or 400m race are not needed because every 100m lane follows the same straight distance. Athletes draw or receive lanes before the race and must run in their allocated lane. The line at the finish is not a target to reach with a hand or a foot; the official placing is decided when the athlete’s torso reaches the vertical plane of the nearer edge of that line.

Because 100m is a sprint, the World Athletics rules require a crouch start and starting blocks for races up to and including 400m. The starter gives the commands ‘On your marks’ and ‘Set’, then fires the gun when the field is steady. Athletes must not begin the start before the report. A false start in an ordinary individual sprint normally disqualifies the athlete responsible; the combined-events procedure is a specific exception. The signal from a start-information system is evidence for the officials, not an automatic ruling by itself.

The first steps are about accelerating out of the blocks, then building toward maximum speed while remaining in the lane. A runner who steps outside the lane can be disqualified under the lane rules, though those rules contain limited exceptions for an accidental contact that gains no material advantage and does not impede another athlete. An athlete cannot gain a legal shortcut by cutting across the track or entering another lane.

At the finish, a photo-finish system compares the torso positions at the line. The head, neck, arms, hands, legs and feet do not decide the order. This is why a runner can lean their chest through the line while their legs are still behind, and why a raised hand does not beat another athlete’s torso. Official timing and placing are recorded separately from the wind reading.

For a 100m race, the wind gauge measures the wind for ten seconds from the starter’s gun. A strong tailwind can help the runners, so World Athletics does not ratify a 100m World Record if the average wind in the direction of running is more than +2.0m/s. That is a record-eligibility rule, not a reason to pretend the race did not happen: the athlete still ran the race and has a finishing place and time, while the mark is classified according to the applicable regulations.

The format around the race depends on the competition. A championship may use heats, a semi-final and a final; a smaller meet may schedule one final or use a different approved format. Qualification places, lane assignments and the number of rounds come from that event’s regulations. The basic race remains the same: a fair start, a full 100m in lane, and the torso across the line first.

In practice

Imagine two sprinters reach the line at almost the same instant. The photo-finish judge does not ask whose foot landed first or whose head was furthest forward. They inspect the image at the finish plane and compare the first point at which each runner’s torso reaches it. The wind reading is then considered separately if a record is being ratified.

Common misunderstandings

  • The first body part across the line wins. The torso decides the finish. A head, arm, hand, leg or foot reaching the plane first does not determine the official placing.
  • A wind-aided race has no valid result. A tailwind above +2.0m/s prevents World Athletics record ratification for the 100m, but it does not erase the race placing or make the event disappear.
  • Any movement in the blocks is automatically a false start. The starter applies the rule to the athlete’s starting action. A start-information system can signal a possible false start, but officials confirm responsibility under the rules.
  • A runner can leave a lane if nobody is beside them. The lane rule still applies; only the rule’s specific no-advantage/no-impediment exceptions can prevent disqualification.

Quick questions

Do 100m runners have to use starting blocks?

Under World Athletics rules, races up to and including 400m use a crouch start and starting blocks. A particular competition’s regulations set the event format.

What part of the body decides a photo finish?

The torso. The head, neck, arms, hands, legs and feet are excluded from the finish judgement.

Does a tailwind over 2.0m/s cancel a 100m win?

No. It affects whether the mark can be ratified as a World Athletics record; it does not cancel the race result.
