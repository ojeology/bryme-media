Knockout football

How is a knockout tie actually decided?

The short answer

If a knockout tie is level after 90 minutes, two 15-minute periods of extra time follow, and if it is still level, a penalty shootout decides it. Some domestic cup competitions still use replays instead — a second match at the other ground.

Knockout football needs a winner on the day, so every competition defines a ladder of tiebreaks. In a two-legged tie the ladder starts with the aggregate score; if that is level, extra time and penalties follow at the end of the second leg. In a single-match tie, extra time comes straight after the 90.

Extra time is 30 minutes split into two halves, with no golden goal. The golden goal — first goal wins, suddenly — was used from 1996 to 2004 and abandoned because it made teams defend desperately rather than attack. Today the whole half is played, and the shootout waits if nobody scores.

The penalty shootout itself has a settled format: five kicks each, taken alternately, then sudden death from the sixth round. Only players who were on the pitch, or are eligible under the competition's rules, may take part. Cup competitions can also keep legacy rules — some domestic cups still schedule replays for drawn ties rather than going straight to extra time, though the biggest cup competitions have moved away from them.

In practice

A cup final finishes 1-1. Extra time produces no goals, so it goes to penalties. Each side scores three of its first five, then the sixth round begins: one miss and one conversion later, the tie is over. The team that 'won' is the one that held its nerve in a contest the regulation scoreline never recorded.

Common misunderstandings

  • "The golden goal still decides close matches." It was abolished in 2004. Extra time is played in full, with penalties as the final tiebreak.
  • "Only the five best penalty takers matter." Shootouts can run to sudden death, and every eligible player may be needed — squads practise far deeper than five.

Quick questions

How long is extra time?

Two halves of 15 minutes. If the tie is still level after 120 minutes, it goes to a penalty shootout.

Do all competitions use replays?

No. Replays are still possible in some domestic cup competitions, but the major European and international tournaments use extra time and penalties instead.
