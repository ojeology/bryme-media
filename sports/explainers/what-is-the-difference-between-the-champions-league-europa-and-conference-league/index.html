Governance & competitions

What is the difference between the Champions League, Europa League and Conference League?

The short answer

They are UEFA's three men's club competitions, ranked by prestige and quality. The Champions League is the elite event for the biggest clubs; the Europa League is the second tier; the Conference League is the third, created in 2021 to give more countries a route into European football. Winning one does not stop you playing another — and there is movement between them.

The Champions League is the one everyone knows: the strongest clubs from the strongest leagues, the largest prize money, and the anthem that European nights are built around. Entry is decided by domestic league position, with the biggest leagues getting several places and smaller ones getting one or none — a system that shapes domestic seasons, because finishing fourth can be worth more than winning a cup.

The Europa League sits beneath it. It takes clubs that finished just outside the Champions League places, domestic cup winners, and — importantly — teams that drop out of the Champions League partway through. Historically that has meant some of the strongest sides in the competition are the ones who started a tier above it, which is why the Europa League's later rounds can look stronger than its reputation suggests.

The Conference League, launched in 2021, is the third tier and exists to widen the map. It gives clubs from smaller associations a realistic route into European group football and a genuine chance of silverware, rather than entering a competition where they are cannon fodder for the elite. Its winners earn a place in the Europa League the following season — the clearest illustration of the ladder.

The ladder is the point: movement runs in both directions. Clubs eliminated from the Champions League drop into the Europa League, and Europa League teams drop into the Conference League, while winning the Conference League or Europa League can promote you to the tier above. Alongside all three sits the Super Cup, played between the Champions League and Europa League winners — a single match that opens the European season.

In practice

A club finishes third in a mid-sized league and enters the Conference League. It wins its group, beats a couple of bigger names, and lifts the trophy — then starts the following season in the Europa League, with the larger prize pool that comes with it. Meanwhile, a giant that finished fourth in the Champions League group stage drops into the Europa League in February and immediately becomes the team to beat.

Common misunderstandings

  • The three competitions are completely separate. Clubs move between them: Champions League teams drop into the Europa League, Europa League teams drop into the Conference League, and winning a lower one can promote you to the tier above.
  • The Europa League is only for small clubs. It regularly contains major clubs that dropped out of the Champions League, especially in the knockout rounds.
  • Any club can enter whichever it likes. Entry is decided by domestic performance and UEFA's access list, which allocates places by country and by finishing position.

Quick questions

Can a club play in more than one in the same season?

Not simultaneously. Clubs move between them: a team eliminated from the Champions League can continue in the Europa League within the same season.

Which is the most valuable to win?

The Champions League, by a wide margin — its prize money and global audience dwarf the other two, though winning the Europa League also brings Champions League qualification.
