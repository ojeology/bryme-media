Match mechanics

How big is a football pitch?

The short answer

A full-size pitch is roughly 105 by 68 metres, but the laws allow a range: 90–120 metres long and 45–90 metres wide, with 100–110 by 64–75 recommended for international matches. The markings inside it — the penalty area, the six-yard box and the ten-yard arc — are fixed in every stadium.

Football is unusual in that it does not insist on a single pitch size. The Laws of the Game give a range for competitive matches: 90 to 120 metres long and 45 to 90 metres wide, with the pitch required to be longer than it is wide. For international matches the range tightens to 100–110 metres by 64–75 metres, which is why the top grounds all feel similar without being identical.

That flexibility is deliberate and tactical. A wide pitch suits a team that wants to stretch the play and use its wingers; a narrow, compact one suits a team that defends in a block and wants to squeeze space. Clubs are allowed to set their dimensions anywhere inside the legal range, and some have been openly accused of narrowing the pitch to suit a style. It is one of the few entirely legal home advantages in the sport.

Inside the boundary, the markings are the same everywhere. The penalty area extends 16.5 metres (18 yards) from each goal post and the same distance into the pitch, and it is where the goalkeeper may handle the ball — the single most important rectangle on the field. Inside it sits the six-yard box, 5.5 metres out, which is the goal-kick zone. The penalty spot is 11 metres from goal, and the arc at the top of the area marks the ten-yard exclusion zone around it.

Everything else is standard too: the centre circle is 9.15 metres in radius — the same ten yards as the free-kick distance — and the goal itself is 7.32 metres wide by 2.44 metres high, dimensions that have barely changed since the laws were codified. The touchlines and goal lines are part of the field of play, which is why a ball resting on the line is still in play, a detail that decides offside and goal-line decisions constantly.

In practice

A club with two flying wingers sets its pitch at the maximum legal width, giving them more room to isolate a full-back one-on-one. A rival that defends narrow and counters quickly sets its pitch at the minimum, compressing the same eleven players into less space. Both are entirely within the laws — the dimensions are a home-club choice inside the permitted range.

Common misunderstandings

  • Every professional pitch is the same size. The laws permit a range of lengths and widths, and clubs choose where inside it to sit. Only international matches have a tight recommended band.
  • If the ball is on the line, it is out. Lines are part of the area they enclose, so a ball resting on the touchline is still in play and a ball on the goal line is not a goal.
  • The penalty arc is just decoration. It marks the ten-yard zone around the penalty spot — players must be outside it when a penalty is taken.

Quick questions

How big is the goal?

7.32 metres wide by 2.44 metres high (8 yards by 8 feet) — dimensions that have been standard since the laws were codified.

Why is the centre circle 9.15 metres?

Because that is ten yards — the same distance opponents must retreat at a free kick. At kick-off it keeps the opposition out of the centre.
