Football numbers

What is xG (expected goals)?

The short answer

Expected goals (xG) measures the quality of a chance: every shot is given a probability between 0 and 1 based on how likely it is to be scored, and those probabilities are added up to compare how good each team's chances were.

A shot's value comes from its circumstances — distance, angle, whether it was struck with the foot or a header, how much pressure the shooter was under, and how the chance was created. A tap-in from six yards might be worth 0.9 xG; a hopeful effort from 30 yards, 0.03. Add every shot together and you get a team's match total.

The number is a description, not a prophecy. A team can 'win' the xG battle comfortably and still lose 1-0 — that is football, not a glitch. What xG is genuinely good at is separating process from result: it tells you whether a team is creating the kind of chances that usually go in, or living on shots that rarely do.

Different providers build different models, so the same match can produce slightly different totals. Most models also say nothing about the shooter: the same chance is worth the same xG whether it falls to a clinical finisher or a centre-back. That limitation is exactly why coaches read xG alongside the eye test, not instead of it.

In practice

A side has 18 shots and loses. The shots were mostly long-range efforts under pressure — total xG, 0.8. The winner had four shots but two of them were close-range, unmarked headers — total xG, 1.6. The scoreline says 0-1, but the xG line says the winner did not create more; they just created better.

Common misunderstandings

  • "The team with more xG deserved to win." xG describes chance quality, not deserts. Finishing, goalkeeping and game state are all outside the number.
  • "xG is a prediction of the score." It is a retrospective measure of the chances that were created — some models are used in forecasting, but the match number itself is a description.

Quick questions

What is a good xG total?

Roughly one goal is an average team's match total, so anything above two means a side created a stream of genuinely dangerous chances. A total below 0.5 usually means the goalkeeper had a quiet day.

Why do two sites disagree about the same shot?

Each provider tunes its own model — different data, different weightings. Treat small differences as noise and look at the season-long picture.
