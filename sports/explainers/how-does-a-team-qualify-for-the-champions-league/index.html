Governance & competitions

How does a team qualify for the Champions League?

The short answer

Clubs qualify through their domestic league position — and, in some countries, by winning a domestic cup or the previous European competition. UEFA allocates a fixed number of places per country using a coefficient ranking, so finishing fourth in England can be worth more than finishing second somewhere else.

The basic route is league position. Each season, UEFA's access list tells every national association how many of its clubs may enter the Champions League, and the league table decides which ones they are. The strongest leagues get four places; mid-ranked leagues get two or three; the smallest get one, often through a qualifying round rather than direct entry.

How many places a country gets is decided by its coefficient — a rolling five-year score built from how its clubs perform in Europe. Countries that do well earn more places; countries that do badly lose them. This is why the distribution shifts slowly over the years, and why a league's overall health in Europe matters to every club in it, not just the ones competing.

There are other doors. Winning the previous season's Champions League guarantees a place regardless of domestic position, and winning the Europa League does the same for its competition — which is how a club having a poor domestic season can still appear in the Champions League. Some associations also route their domestic cup winners into Europe, though usually into the Europa League or Conference League rather than the Champions League itself.

The distinction between entering and being in matters. Clubs from smaller leagues must survive qualifying rounds played in the summer before the competition proper begins; clubs from the top leagues enter directly into the league phase, which is why the phrase 'direct qualification' carries so much financial weight. Every round survived is prize money earned, and the gap between a club that reaches the league phase and one that exits in qualifying can be tens of millions in revenue.

In practice

Two clubs finish third. One plays in a league ranked in UEFA's top four and goes straight into the Champions League league phase. The other plays in a league ranked twelfth and must win two qualifying ties in July and August just to reach the same starting point — with the same prize money on the line and far less preparation time. Same finishing position, entirely different routes.

Common misunderstandings

  • The league champion of every country qualifies automatically. Most do, but clubs from smaller associations often have to come through qualifying rounds, and the number of places per country varies with its coefficient.
  • Only league position counts. Winning the previous Champions League or Europa League also grants entry, regardless of where the club finished domestically.
  • The number of places per country is fixed. It changes with that country's five-year coefficient — strong collective performances earn more berths, poor ones lose them.

Quick questions

Can a cup win get a club into the Champions League?

Usually the domestic cup leads to the Europa League or Conference League. The exceptions are winning the Champions League or Europa League itself, both of which grant entry to the following season's Champions League.

What are qualifying rounds?

Summer knockout ties that clubs from lower-ranked associations must win to reach the league phase. Each round carries prize money and can make a significant difference to a club's finances.
