Knockout football

What does aggregate score mean in football?

The short answer

An aggregate score is the combined result of a two-legged tie: add both legs together and the team with the higher total advances. A 2-0 home win followed by a 1-3 away defeat makes the tie 3-3 on aggregate — which then needs another way to be settled.

Two-legged ties exist so that both clubs get a home match. The scores are carried over rather than reset: if you win the first leg 2-0 and lose the second 0-1, you go through 2-1 on aggregate. Broadcasters write it as '2-1 agg' to make clear that the second leg's scoreline alone is not the result of the tie.

When the aggregate score is level, the tie needs a tiebreak. Since 2021-22 in UEFA competitions that means 30 extra minutes and, if still level, penalties at the end of the second leg. Before that, the away goals rule — goals scored on the road counting double in a level tie — decided it, which is why older match reports talk about away goals so much.

Not every knockout round works this way. World Cup and European Championship knockout matches are single games. Domestic league cups tend to be single games too, while continental club competitions and many playoff rounds use two legs. The format is a competition choice, not a universal law.

In practice

In a Champions League semi-final, a team wins the home leg 1-0, then goes 2-0 down away. At that moment the aggregate is 2-1 to the opponents — the trailing side needs one goal to force extra time and two to win the tie outright. Every goal in the second leg swings both the match and the aggregate at the same time, which is what makes two-legged football so tense.

Common misunderstandings

  • "Winning both legs is the only way to be comfortable." Squads routinely advance with a loss in one leg. The only number that matters is the combined score.
  • "Away goals still decide level ties in Europe." Not since 2021-22. UEFA replaced them with extra time and penalties at the end of the second leg.

Quick questions

Does away goals apply in the first leg?

No. The away goals rule only ever mattered when the aggregate was level after both legs — and it no longer applies in UEFA club competitions at all.

Which competitions still use two-legged ties?

UEFA club competitions through the semi-finals, many World Cup and Euro qualifying play-offs, and domestic playoff rounds. Tournament knockout finals are single matches.
