What is UEFA — and what does it actually do?

The short answer

UEFA is European football's governing body: a confederation of 55 national associations that runs the Champions League, Europa League, Conference League and the European Championship, licenses clubs, sets financial rules for its competitions, and represents European football inside FIFA. It does not write the Laws of the Game — that is IFAB's job.

UEFA was founded in 1954 and is based in Nyon, Switzerland. Its members are national associations — England's FA, Spain's RFEF, Nigeria's NFF is NOT a member (that is CAF territory) — each of which runs its own domestic football. UEFA sits one level above them: everything that involves clubs or countries from different European nations falls under its competitions, regulations and disciplinary system.

That layer covers a huge amount. UEFA admits clubs to its competitions and seeds the draws using its own coefficient system, appoints referees for its matches, runs the disciplinary process when something goes wrong, distributes the prize money, and enforces financial rules — the framework fans know as financial fair play, now folded into squad-cost and sustainability rules. When a club is thrown out of Europe for breaching those rules, it is a UEFA decision.

UEFA also runs the European Championship (the Euros) for national teams, the Nations League and youth and women's tournaments, and it certifies stadiums through its licensing system. For national teams, its members' results feed FIFA's world ranking; for clubs, its competitions are where most European prize money is made.

What UEFA does not do is set the rules of the sport itself. Offside, handball, cards and the rest are written by IFAB, and FIFA governs the global game. Think of the layers as: IFAB writes the laws, each national association runs its own league, UEFA governs the European competitions and regulations, and FIFA sits at the top for the world game.

In practice

When a club wins its domestic league and enters the Champions League, three different bodies touch the journey. The national league confirms it qualified; UEFA admits it, seeds it into a pot and pays its prize money; and if the club then breaks UEFA's financial rules, a UEFA judicial body — not the domestic league — handles the case. The domestic league still controls the club's own competition, its fixtures and its points.

Common misunderstandings

  • UEFA makes the rules of football. IFAB writes the Laws of the Game. UEFA applies them and writes the regulations for its own competitions.
  • UEFA and FIFA are the same thing at different sizes. They are separate bodies with separate memberships: FIFA governs the world game and the World Cup; UEFA is the European confederation inside FIFA, running European competitions like the Champions League and Euros.

Quick questions

How many countries are in UEFA?

55 member associations, covering the whole European region — from the giants like England, Spain and Germany to small states like Gibraltar and San Marino, plus Israel and Kazakhstan, which compete in European football by choice.

Does UEFA run domestic leagues like the Premier League?

No. Domestic leagues are run by each country's own association. UEFA governs European club competitions, national-team tournaments and the regulations that apply across its region.
