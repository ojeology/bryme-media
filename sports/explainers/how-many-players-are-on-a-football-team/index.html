Match mechanics

How many players are on a football team?

The short answer

Eleven players start a match, one of whom must be a goalkeeper. A team can finish with as few as seven — below that the match is abandoned — and the squad behind them is far larger: a matchday squad is typically twenty, with the rest of the registered squad unused.

Eleven became the standard in the 19th century and has been fixed in the Laws of the Game ever since. The number is arbitrary in the way that most sporting conventions are — early matches varied enormously — but once the Laws were codified, eleven stuck, and everything from pitch dimensions to formation shapes has been designed around it since.

One of the eleven has to be a goalkeeper, and only the goalkeeper may handle the ball inside his own penalty area. If a team has no goalkeeper available — because he was sent off and no substitutes remain, or an outfield player had to take the gloves after a substitution — the team is playing without one, which is legal but tactically desperate. Players may swap roles with the referee's permission during a stoppage.

The floor is seven. A team reduced below seven players — through multiple dismissals or injuries with no substitutions left — cannot continue, and the match is abandoned and forfeited. Referees have no discretion here: it is a hard limit in the law rather than a judgement call, which is why six red cards in one match effectively ends the contest.

Behind the eleven on the pitch sits a much larger group. Clubs register squads of twenty-five or more for a season, name a matchday squad of around twenty, and are typically allowed to use five of the substitutes on the bench. So a professional club's 'team' is closer to thirty players, with eleven on the field at any moment.

In practice

A club registers twenty-five players for the season, names twenty for a Saturday match, and uses five of them from the bench. Eleven start, eleven finish — but sixteen players from that club have touched the ball in a competitive match by the final whistle, and the other nine trained all week for nothing.

Common misunderstandings

  • A team must always have eleven players on the pitch. Eleven start, but a team can legally finish with seven. Below seven, the match is abandoned.
  • The goalkeeper is optional. A team must have one player in goal at all times. If no specialist is available, an outfield player takes the role.
  • The squad and the team are the same eleven. Clubs register twenty-five or more players and name around twenty on a matchday; eleven of those are on the pitch at kick-off.

Quick questions

Can a team start a match with fewer than eleven?

Yes, if it has no eligible players available — the match can start with ten or even the minimum seven, though the short-handed side is at a severe disadvantage and may face disciplinary consequences.

Why eleven?

It was settled when the Laws were codified in the 19th century and became the global standard; there is no tactical or mathematical reason it could not have been another number.
