Football laws

How does VAR actually work?

The short answer

VAR is a video referee who can look again at four match-changing situations — goals, penalty decisions, direct red cards and mistaken identity — and recommend a review only for a clear and obvious error or a serious missed incident. The on-field referee always makes the final call.

The video team watches the match live with the same broadcast pictures, plus calibrated offside cameras. When something in one of the four reviewable categories looks wrong, they tell the referee — but they do not decide the outcome. The referee can accept a factual correction, or walk to the pitchside monitor and judge for themselves.

The four categories matter because they explain most VAR arguments. A missed second yellow card cannot be reviewed. A foul outside the box cannot become a penalty. And for offside, the decision is treated as factual once the calibrated lines or semi-automated system place the players — which is why offside reviews feel colder than handball debates.

VAR was used at a World Cup for the first time in 2018 and reached the Premier League in 2019-20. The trade-off has been the same everywhere: fewer clear errors, but longer stoppages, and goals celebrated with half an eye on the video referee.

In practice

A goal is scored, the stadium celebrates — then the referee signals that the buildup is being checked. If the video team finds the scorer's teammate was fractionally offside in the build-up, the goal is disallowed and play restarts with a free kick. The crowd sees the same replay lines seconds later, which is why broadcast graphics matter as much as the decision itself.

Common misunderstandings

  • "VAR can review anything that looks wrong." Only four categories: goals, penalties, direct red cards and mistaken identity. Everything else is off-limits.
  • "The VAR referee overrules the on-field referee." They can only recommend a review. The referee in the middle keeps the final decision — unless the correction is purely factual, like offside.

Quick questions

Can VAR award a penalty for something the referee missed?

Yes — a serious missed incident inside one of the four categories can be reviewed, including a penalty that was not given.

Why do we sometimes see no replay on the big screen?

In-stadium rules differ by competition and some leagues restrict which replays can be shown. The referee's final signal is the binding one either way.
