Basketball rules

What is goaltending in basketball?

The short answer

Goaltending is touching the ball on its way down towards the basket, or while it is on or above the rim and could still go in. The shot is awarded as if it had gone in. The related offence of basket interference is touching the rim, net or ball while the shot is still live on the rim.

The principle is that a shot in flight is sacred. Once the ball is on its downward flight towards the basket and has a chance of going in, nobody may touch it — not a defender pinning it against the backboard, not a team-mate tapping it in to be helpful. The whistle goes and the basket is awarded: two points, or three if it was a three-point attempt, regardless of where the ball was touched.

Timing is everything. A block is legal while the ball is still rising or at the apex — this is why spectacular chase-down blocks are allowed and why players aim to meet the ball at its highest point. The moment it starts descending towards the rim, touching it becomes goaltending. Hitting the ball after it has already touched the backboard on the way down is goaltending too, which is why defenders learn to go for the ball before it reaches the glass.

Basket interference is the close cousin. It covers touching the ball while it is on or within the rim's cylinder, touching the rim or net while the ball is on or in it, or reaching up through the hoop to play the ball. On offence it is a violation that wipes out the basket; on defence it is a violation that awards the basket. Slapping the backboard so hard that the rim shakes and the ball rattles out is interference as well — the rim is part of the protected space.

The punishment reflects which side offended. Defensive goaltending or interference awards the points the shot would have been worth. Offensive goaltending or interference is a violation: no basket, possession to the other team. Officials make the call from angles that fans rarely see on television, and because the difference between a clean block and goaltending can be a few centimetres, it is one of the most replayed decisions in the sport.

In practice

A defender leaps and pins the ball against the glass just as it is about to drop through — goaltending, and the basket counts even though he 'saved' it. Now take the same play a fraction earlier, with the ball still rising: the block is clean and legal. The entire difference is whether the ball had begun its descent, which is why the ruling is almost always reviewed.

Common misunderstandings

  • Any block above the rim is goaltending. Only touching the ball on its downward flight, or when it is on or above the rim and could go in. A block at the apex is clean.
  • Goaltending only applies to defenders. Offensive goaltending exists too — tapping a team-mate's shot in while it is on the rim is a violation and the basket is wiped out.
  • You can slap the backboard to make the ball miss. Doing so while the ball is live on the rim is basket interference, and the basket is awarded to the shooting team.

Quick questions

What is the difference between goaltending and basket interference?

Goaltending is touching a shot on its downward flight. Basket interference is touching the ball, rim or net while the ball is on or in the rim's cylinder, or reaching up through the hoop.

Can an offensive player tip in a missed shot?

Yes, once the ball has left the rim's cylinder or is no longer on the rim. Tipping it in while it is still on or directly above the ring is offensive goaltending.
