Basketball rules

What is a double dribble?

The short answer

A double dribble is dribbling the ball with both hands at the same time, or stopping your dribble — by holding the ball — and then starting to dribble again. Once the dribble has ended, the player must pass or shoot.

A dribble is a single continuous action: the player taps, pushes or throws the ball to the floor and it comes back to his hand. The dribble ends when he touches the ball with both hands simultaneously, when he permits it to come to rest in one or both hands, or when he catches it. At that instant, the option to dribble again is gone — he has to pass or shoot, and starting a new dribble is the violation everyone calls a double dribble.

Dribbling with two hands at once is the other half of the rule. A player may use either hand and may switch between them, but he cannot push the ball down with both hands together, which is why a 'two-handed dribble' is whistled immediately. A related violation is carrying, or palming — letting the ball come to rest in the hand and turning it over, so the dribble becomes a cradle rather than a tap. Both are judged on whether the hand got underneath the ball.

Why the rule matters is tactical. A player who has picked up his dribble is a committed player: he cannot drive, he cannot create his own space, and the defence knows it. That is exactly why defences trap ball-handlers and why the phrase 'pick up your dribble' is coaching shorthand for a mistake. Once the dribble is gone, the player has to make a decision with defenders converging.

The punishment is loss of possession, like travelling — a violation rather than a personal foul, so no free throws and nothing recorded against the player. The exception is that a team in the bonus does not shoot free throws for a violation; they simply inbound the ball. Officials judge the hand position closely, which is why high, lazy dribbles attract whistles and low, hard ones do not.

In practice

A guard picks up his dribble in the corner, realises he is trapped, panics and throws the ball at the floor to escape. Whistle — double dribble, turnover. Had he kept the ball in one hand and pivoted, he could have passed out of the trap legally. The violation is not the movement; it is restarting a dribble that had already ended.

Common misunderstandings

  • You can dribble again as long as nobody touched the ball. No — once you end your dribble by holding or catching the ball, dribbling again is a violation regardless of whether anyone else touched it.
  • Switching hands mid-dribble is a double dribble. Switching hands is legal. It is only illegal to dribble with both hands simultaneously, or to stop and restart.
  • A double dribble sends the other team to the free-throw line. It is a violation, not a foul — the defence simply inbounds the ball from the sideline.

Quick questions

What is carrying or palming?

Letting the ball rest in your hand and turning it over during a dribble, so the hand is underneath rather than on top of the ball. It is a violation in the same family as the double dribble.

Can you dribble again after a defender knocks the ball away?

Yes — if the defender's touch caused you to lose control, the dribble has not ended by your own action, so you may dribble again once you regain it.
