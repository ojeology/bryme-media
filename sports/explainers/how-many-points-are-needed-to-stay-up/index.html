League rules

How many points are needed to stay up?

The short answer

Forty points is the traditional safety target in a 38-game league, but it is a rule of thumb rather than a guarantee — the real number is whatever the third-from-bottom team finishes with, and teams have both survived and been relegated on 40.

The 'forty points' idea comes from simple arithmetic: roughly 1.05 points per game across a season. In a 38-match league that usually puts a team clear of the bottom three, because the relegation places are normally decided by who collapses rather than who merely struggles. Managers repeat it because it gives a frightened dressing room a target that feels achievable.

But the actual threshold moves every season with the strength of the bottom of the table. In seasons where three teams are dreadful, the safety line drops into the mid-thirties. In seasons where the bottom half is tight — every team beating the others consistently — the line rises, and 40 has not been enough. This is why the number is best read as 'beat the third-worst team', not 'reach 40'.

Goal difference is the tiebreaker waiting underneath all of it, and it acts as a hidden points handicap. A team whose goal difference is twenty worse than a rival effectively needs an extra win to be level. That is why teams in deep trouble often prioritise stopping the bleeding over chasing unlikely wins, and why a heavy defeat in March can feel like relegation even though the points column barely moves.

The maths of survival are also the maths of timing: the points needed are not the points already banked but the projected finish of the teams below. A good way to read the run-in is to look at the fixtures of the team in eighteenth, not your own — survival is relative, which is the part that makes the end of a season so strange.

In practice

A team on 34 points with five matches left looks doomed on paper. But if the team in eighteenth is on 30 with a brutal run of fixtures against the top four, and the nineteenth is eight points adrift with no wins in two months, 36 might be enough — the target is not a number, it is the team below you failing to reach it.

Common misunderstandings

  • Forty points guarantees survival. It is a strong rule of thumb, not a guarantee — teams have gone down on 40 or more when the bottom of the table was unusually competitive.
  • The relegation places are decided only on the final day. They are usually decided by a season of accumulated goal difference and points; the final day only confirms it.
  • Winning the last few matches is all that matters. What matters is what the teams below you do in the same matches — survival is relative, not absolute.

Quick questions

Have teams survived with fewer than 40 points?

Yes. When the bottom of the table is weak, the safety line regularly falls into the mid-thirties; the record for the lowest survival total in the Premier League era is well below 40.

Why do pundits still say forty points then?

Because it is a useful psychological target that historically works in the overwhelming majority of seasons — it is just not a mathematical guarantee.
