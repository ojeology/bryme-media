How does a 4×100m relay work?

The short answer

A 4×100m relay is one lap run by four sprinters, each carrying a baton for one leg and handing it to the next teammate. The baton must be transferred inside a marked 30m takeover zone. The zone rule is about the baton’s position during the exchange, and an athlete must begin their outgoing run within the zone; a transfer outside it can disqualify the team.

In a standard 4×100m relay, four athletes run one 100m leg each, so the team covers 400m in total. The first runner starts with the baton; every later runner must receive it and carry it before making the next handover. Because the track is one lap, the starts are staggered across lanes so that the assigned lanes still represent the same distance. In the 4×100m, each team keeps its lane through the exchanges and around the lap.

The takeover zone is the most important detail. Under World Athletics Technical Rule 24.3, each 4×100m takeover zone is 30m long. The zone has a marked scratch line, positioned 20m from the beginning of the zone under the rule. Runners and officials should use the markings for that track; old diagrams showing a separate acceleration zone can be out of date for the current World Athletics format.

The exchange is judged by the baton, not by where the athletes’ bodies are. The pass begins when the incoming runner’s teammate—the receiving, outgoing runner—first touches the baton. It is complete when the baton is held only by the receiving runner. The whole baton must be inside the takeover zone for the transfer. If the handover starts before the baton is in the zone or is not complete before the baton leaves it, the team can be disqualified.

The outgoing runner has to start their movement within the zone, not from beyond it. The two runners try to match speed so the incoming athlete can deliver the baton without stopping and the outgoing athlete can accelerate safely. Teams practise a visual or verbal cue for the outgoing runner, but the cue is a team technique, not a rule that changes the zone or the legal moment of transfer.

A baton exchange can fail even when the runners appear to cross paths inside the painted box. The official must follow the baton’s position and the actual moment of contact, not just the receiver’s feet or the athletes’ hips. That is why the baton can be passed legally while runners’ bodies are outside a part of the box, or illegally even if both runners seem to be in the right place.

The final runner must carry the baton to the finish. A dropped baton is not automatically a disqualification in every situation, but the athlete must recover it under the relay rules without reducing the distance to be run or obstructing another team. The simplest way to understand the race is: each athlete runs their assigned leg, the baton changes hands wholly inside each zone, and the team finishes with the baton.

In practice

Suppose the outgoing runner begins accelerating as the incoming runner approaches. The legal handover is not decided by the outgoing runner’s foot crossing the start of the box. Officials look at the baton: the exchange starts when the receiver first touches it and ends when the baton is solely in that runner’s hand. Both parts must happen within the 30m zone.

Common misunderstandings

  • The runners’ feet must both be inside the box at the handover. The baton’s position is decisive for the transfer. The rules separately require the outgoing runner to begin movement within the takeover zone.
  • A 4×100m takeover zone is only 20m long under current World Athletics rules. The current World Athletics Technical Rules specify a 30m zone for the 4×100m. Older materials may show a different zone and acceleration-area layout.
  • The outgoing runner can start outside the zone as long as the baton arrives inside it. The outgoing athlete must start within the takeover zone, and the transfer itself must also be completed there.

Quick questions

How long is a 4×100m takeover zone?

World Athletics Technical Rule 24.3 specifies a 30m takeover zone for the 4×100m relay.

Does the receiver’s foot need to be inside the zone?

The position of the baton is decisive for the transfer. Separately, the outgoing runner must begin their movement within the zone.

What happens if the baton is passed outside the zone?

The team can be disqualified for an exchange outside the takeover zone. Officials judge the exchange by the whole baton and when the transfer starts and finishes.
