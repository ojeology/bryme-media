Basketball rules

What is a technical foul?

The short answer

A technical foul is a non-contact offence — arguing with officials, delay of game, calling an illegal timeout, taunting, or hanging on the rim. It costs a free throw for the opposition plus possession, and repeated technicals lead to ejection.

The word 'technical' does the work: this is a foul against the administration of the game rather than against a player. No contact is required. Dissent towards an official, delaying the restart, requesting a timeout a team does not have, entering the court illegally, taunting an opponent, or hanging on the rim after a dunk can all be technicals. The official signals it with two hands forming a T.

The penalty is deliberately harsher than an ordinary foul, because the offence is about conduct rather than a basketball play. In the NBA a technical brings one free throw for the opposing team, plus possession of the ball — so a moment of dissent can be a two-possession swing in a close game. Under FIBA rules a technical brings two free throws plus possession. Either way, the offending player is charged with it personally, and accumulating them brings escalating penalties.

Accumulation is where careers are affected. In the NBA, two technical fouls in a game mean an automatic ejection; technicals accumulate across the regular season, with suspensions after a set number and fines that escalate as the total rises. In playoff basketball the thresholds are lower, which is why stars who are known for arguing with referees are carefully managed by their coaches in the post-season.

There is a separate category for dangerous contact: the flagrant foul in the NBA, or unsportsmanlike foul under FIBA. Those are contact fouls that are unnecessary or excessive, punished with free throws plus possession and, for the most serious grade, ejection. The line between them is intent — a technical is about behaviour and procedure, a flagrant is about how a player used his body — and officials are instructed to judge the intent behind the act rather than merely the outcome.

In practice

A player disagrees with a call, turns and says something to the official he should not: whistle, technical foul, one free throw for the other team and the ball as well. In a game decided by two points, that is the game. Multiply it across a season and the same player is fined repeatedly, suspended for a match, and eventually known more for the argument than for the basketball.

Common misunderstandings

  • A technical foul involves contact. It is specifically a non-contact offence — conduct, procedure or delay. Contact offences are personal or flagrant fouls.
  • It costs the same as any other foul. It costs a free throw plus possession, and accumulating them brings ejections, fines and suspensions.
  • Two technicals in a season get you ejected. Two in a single game mean ejection in the NBA; the season-long totals bring fines and suspensions at higher thresholds.

Quick questions

What is the penalty for a technical foul?

One free throw plus possession in the NBA, or two free throws plus possession under FIBA rules. Any opposing player may take the shots.

How many technicals before an ejection?

Two technical fouls in the same game mean an automatic ejection in the NBA, and a flagrant two is an immediate ejection on its own.
