Movies Like Dune: Part Two

Dune: Part Two is not just a sequel — it is a statement about size: deserts that take minutes to cross on screen, a score you feel in your chest, and a plot that trades comfort for inevitability. These five films scratch different parts of that same itch.

What makes the Dune films unusual is their confidence in slowness. Whole sequences play out with almost no dialogue; the politics is carried by faces and landscapes. Audiences who love that usually know exactly what they are chasing: films where production design is a language, not decoration.

So this list mixes three kinds of pick — the same director's thinking sci-fi, epics built on practical spectacle, and one film included purely for its filmmaking method. Every recommendation links to its BRYME title page for trailer, cast and viewing details.

Who this list is for

For viewers who left Part Two wanting weight rather than speed: big-canvas science fiction, deliberate pacing, and craft you can point at. If you mainly loved the sandworms and action, Mad Max: Fury Road is your first stop on the list.

The list — 5 to queue next

each pick links to its BRYME title page
Film · 2017

Blade Runner 2049

Villeneuve's other future. A two-hour-and-forty-minute meditation on identity that is also the most beautiful film on this list — same patience, same faith that a long silent shot of a landscape can do more than a monologue. If Dune's visual language was what held you, start here.

Blade Runner 2049 on BRYME — trailer and details →

Film · 2014

Interstellar

The emotional version of the Dune formula: enormous science-fiction concepts — time, gravity, survival — anchored to a father and daughter. Where Dune goes mythic, Interstellar goes sentimental, and the combination of practical effects and Zimmer-scale sound is the direct ancestor of Villeneuve's desert.

Interstellar on BRYME — cast, trailer and where to watch →

Film · 2016

Arrival

Also Villeneuve, also science fiction, and the film most likely to convert a Dune fan into a Villeneuve completionist. A first-contact story told through language and grief, it proves his scale does not depend on desert planets — just on patience and a devastating final act.

Arrival on BRYME — the thinking viewer's first contact →

Film · 2015

Mad Max: Fury Road

The other desert. Nearly all practical stunts, almost no dialogue for its first act, and a chase that never really stops — this is what maximalist immersion looks like when the restraint comes off. Pair it with Dune: Part Two and you have both poles of modern epic cinema.

Mad Max: Fury Road on BRYME — the practical-effects epic →

Film · 2019

1917

Included for method rather than genre. Sam Mendes and Roger Deakins staged an entire war film to look like a single continuous take, so the audience never gets permission to look away. Different subject, same goal as Dune's best sequences: pinning you to the screen and refusing to cut.

How to watch

Blade Runner 2049, Interstellar, Arrival, 1917 and Dune: Part Two are all widely available on major streaming rentals and several subscription libraries; availability rotates constantly, so check the BRYME title pages for trailer and current streaming options. Fury Road is the same — rentable everywhere, and worth seeking out in the best picture quality you can find, since all five films here are, frankly, half about image and sound.

Questions, quickly

Which one is most like Dune: Part Two in style?

Blade Runner 2049 — same director, same pacing philosophy, same obsession with scale and silhouette.

Do I need to watch these in order?

No. They are unrelated films. If you want a sequence, start with Arrival and Blade Runner 2049, then save Fury Road for when you want the version without the patience.

Any lighter options for the same feeling?

The Martian trades awe for problem-solving, but this list is deliberately built around films that take their own time.
