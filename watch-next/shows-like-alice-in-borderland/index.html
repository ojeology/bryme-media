Shows Like Alice in Borderland

Alice in Borderland drops a group of Tokyo friends into an emptied city where survival is a series of brutal games, and leaves you with a very specific craving: more stories where ordinary people are forced to think their way out of impossible rules.

The show works because it respects its own game design. Cards set the difficulty, teams form and fracture, and the tension comes less from monsters than from the arithmetic of who survives. When it ends, the feeling you want more of is not gore — it is that particular blend of strategy, dread and uneasy teamwork.

So the five shows below are not simply 'foreign survival series'. Each one is here for a specific reason: the same engine of rules and stakes, the same pressure on a group, or the same willingness to kill characters you had just started to like. Every pick links to its full BRYME title page, so you can check the facts before you commit an evening.

Who this list is for

This list is for viewers who liked the game logic more than the body count — people who enjoyed working out the wins alongside Arisu more than they enjoyed the shocks. If you want pure horror instead, two of the picks below (Sweet Home, Kingdom) push further in that direction; the others stay closer to thriller.

Series · 2021

Squid Game

The obvious cousin, and still the sharpest one. Debt-ridden strangers play childhood games for survival, with the same escalating dread and the same question underneath: what is a human life worth to the people running the show? It trades Borderland's manga surrealism for class-war satire, and the second season widens the world rather than resetting it.

Series · 2022

All of Us Are Dead

A zombie outbreak confined to a single Korean high school. Where Alice spreads its cast across a whole city, this one compresses the disaster into corridors, classrooms and rooftops, so the social hierarchy of the school becomes part of the survival problem. It is the most claustrophobic pick on this list.

Series · 2020

Sweet Home

Residents of an apartment block turn into monsters that embody their private desires — and the show's best trick is making you unsure which survivors are still people. If the part of Borderland you loved was the creeping suspicion of your own group, this is that feeling with better monsters.

Series · 2019

Kingdom

Zombies in Joseon-era Korea, wrapped inside a political thriller. The plague is real, but the more dangerous enemy is the court. It is the most 'adult' show here — slower, more strategic, and structured like a conspiracy story that happens to be overrun by the dead.

Series · 2021

Yellowjackets

The Western entry: a girls' football team survives a plane crash in the wilderness, and the story runs on two timelines — the teenagers then, the damaged adults now. It replaces games and monsters with something colder: the slow realisation that the group is inventing its own rules to survive.

How to watch

Availability follows rights, so it changes country by country and month by month — the four Korean shows here stream on Netflix in most regions, while Yellowjackets sits on Showtime in the US and Paramount+ in several other markets. Questions, quickly

Is Alice in Borderland finished?

The first two seasons resolve their central arc, and Netflix has continued the franchise since. Check your region's catalogue for the current season list — announcements outrun availability constantly.

Which of these is closest to Alice in Borderland season 1?

Squid Game. It has the same death-game structure and the same fascination with what desperation does to group dynamics — just without the manga logic.

Any of these for younger viewers?

None of them are family viewing. Sweet Home, All of Us Are Dead and Kingdom all carry strong violence ratings, and Yellowjackets deals with darker material than the others.
