Shows Like Squid Game

Squid Game built its reputation on one irresistible idea: childhood games, adult stakes, 456 strangers, and a system designed so that the players eliminate each other. The shows below all put a group under that kind of pressure — some with actual games, some with a heist, some with nothing but wilderness.

What people remember about Squid Game is rarely a single death. It is the machinery: the pink guards, the voting, the way hope itself became a resource. The best companion viewing keeps that focus on systems and group psychology rather than simply copying the arena.

Each recommendation below is here for a reason — one shares the game architecture, one the apocalyptic pressure, one the team-under-impossible-odds thrill — and every one links to its own BRYME title page so you can check cast, trailer and availability before you start.

Who this list is for

These suit viewers who liked the social experiment of Squid Game more than its shocks — the episodes where the characters argue about whether to continue are, for this list's purposes, the best ones. All five are adult dramas with heavy content; none is casual background viewing.

The list — 5 to queue next

each pick links to its BRYME title page
Series · 2020

Alice in Borderland

The nearest neighbour in spirit: strangers in an emptied Tokyo forced through games whose rules they must reverse-engineer to live. Where Squid Game's games are children's playgrounds turned lethal, Borderland's are puzzles with difficulty cards — but the group dynamics rot in exactly the same way.

Alice in Borderland on BRYME — games, cast and seasons →

Series · 2022

All of Us Are Dead

A zombie outbreak traps a school, and survival becomes a matter of alliances, barricades and leadership — the same questions of who is worth saving, compressed into one building. It is Squid Game's human arithmetic with a faster clock.

All of Us Are Dead on BRYME — the outbreak, explained →

Series · 2020

Sweet Home

Apartment residents versus monsters born from their own desires. The show's engine is distrust: every survivor might be turning, and the rules for staying human keep shifting. If Squid Game's voting scenes were your favourite part, this is an entire series of them.

Sweet Home on BRYME — monsters and seasons →

Series · 2017

Money Heist

The last pick swaps the death game for a heist: eight strangers, one Professor, and a plan that assumes the group will try to out-think the police for days. It is the warmest show on this list — until it is not — and the closest any of these comes to a team you actually root for.

Money Heist on BRYME — the Professor's plan →

Series · 2021

Yellowjackets

A team survives a plane crash and slowly invents its own society, and the series shows the results decades later in the survivors' wrecked adult lives. No games, no guards — just the long-term cost of what a group does to stay alive, which is Squid Game's real subject.

How to watch

Four of these are Netflix originals in most regions; Yellowjackets is a US cable series that streams on Paramount+ in many markets and Showtime in the US. Since rights move, use the linked BRYME title pages for a current availability check rather than trusting any list — including this one.

Questions, quickly

Is any of these lighter than Squid Game?

Not really — but Money Heist is the closest to 'fun'. It is a thriller first, with far less on-screen death than the others.

Where should I start if I want more actual games?

Alice in Borderland. It is the only show here built around the same death-game architecture as Squid Game.

Do I need to watch these in any order?

No. They are independent series; this list is ordered by closeness to Squid Game's core premise, not chronology.
