## Part 2 — First-hand experience

You said all three areas. For each, I need **specifics, not summaries**. The test: could a
stranger writing from Google have produced this sentence? If yes, it's not useful. If it
contains a number, a delay, a name or a mistake, it is.

### Examples — the difference this makes

> **Unusable (generic, could be anyone):**
> "Freelancing takes patience and consistency. Build a strong profile and deliver quality
> work, and clients will come."

> **Usable (only you could write it):**
> "My first Fiverr order was $15 for a logo. It cleared 14 days after delivery, then the
> Payoneer transfer took another 2 days, so it was almost three weeks before I could spend
> it. I'd priced the gig assuming I'd be paid that week."

The second one needs no embellishment. That is the entire difference between content that
ranks and content that gets ignored.
