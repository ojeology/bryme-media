Squid Game

  • Year 2021
  • Country South Korea
  • Director Hwang Dong-hyeok
  • Starring Lee Jung-jae, Lee Byung-hun, Wi Ha-joon
  • Genre Survival, Thriller, Drama

Hwang Dong-hyeok's Squid Game took a Korean childhood game called squid — where one team defends a shape against another — and turned it into the structure of a global hit. Four hundred and fifty-six players, all in ruinous debt, are invited to compete in a series of playground games. Winners advance. Losers are eliminated in the most literal sense.

The show's true subject is not the games but the queue: who joins, why they stay, and how the outside world's debts make a death match look rational. The masked organisers are rarely on screen, and never needed to be — the players eliminate each other perfectly well themselves.

Why it is worth your evening

Its iconography did the heavy lifting — the guard colours, the outfits, the doll — but the reason it holds up is structure: a clear premise, episodic games, and a cast large enough that any character can leave at any moment. The later seasons widen the question of who is running the games rather than simply replaying them, which is a rarer move than it sounds.

How to watch it

Netflix original worldwide. Korean-language production with subtitles; dubbed audio is available in many regions. Strong violence and mature themes throughout — this is not family viewing despite the playground imagery.
