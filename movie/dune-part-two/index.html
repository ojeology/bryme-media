Denis Villeneuve's second Dune film picks up exactly where the first ended: Paul Atreides alive among the Fremen, his house destroyed, and a desert war waiting to be lit. What follows is not a rise-and-fall story so much as a study of inevitability — the audience sees the Holy War coming long before Paul lets himself admit he is the one carrying it.

At 166 minutes it earns its patience: sequence after sequence is carried by image, sound and silence rather than exposition. Hans Zimmer's score does as much world-building as the production design, and the film treats the desert itself as the antagonist.

Why it is worth your evening

It is blockbuster filmmaking with an unusual level of conviction — Villeneuve refuses the easy catharsis at almost every turn, and the result is a film that feels bigger on a second viewing, not smaller. Timothée Chalamet's performance holds the centre while Zendaya, Rebecca Ferguson and Austin Butler define what kind of movie this is around him.

How to watch it

Rentable or streaming on major services depending on your region; streaming rights move regularly, so check current listings. Watch the first Dune beforehand — Part Two starts mid-story and never recaps.
