Quick summary

Christopher Nolan’s Interstellar sends Cooper off-world to find humanity another home while Murph grows up inside the problem he left behind. It is a science-fiction film that keeps circling love, time, and dust. BRYME already has a full ending explainer and a ‘movies like this’ guide if the three hours are not enough.

What Interstellar is about

The mission is the advertisement. The film’s real subject is what it costs to leave — and whether love is a force the plot is allowed to take seriously. The science talk is loud; the father-daughter line is louder.

If you only remember the docking or the wave, you remember the set pieces. The reason it stays on BRYME’s popular list is the last act’s stubborn sincerity. We explain that ending in its own article rather than stuffing it here.

Notes

  • Hans Zimmer wrote the score before seeing the script, based on a one-page description.
  • The wormhole and black hole visuals were generated with real physics equations.
  • The cornfield scenes were filmed in a real field that had just been harvested for the production.
  • The TARS robot was operated by puppeteers, not CGI.
