Five Nigerian Thrillers to Start With, and What Each One Does Differently

Nigerian thrillers to start with: five Nollywood titles on BRYME — political mystery, folklore, crime — and what each one actually does.

Nigerian thrillers to start with

Nigerian thriller cinema is not one style. The five below are already in the BRYME catalogue. Pick the mood. This is not a claim that Nollywood is only crime, and not a fake earnings chart. If you want the wider Lagos family-crime lane after this, A Tribe Called Judah and King of Boys sit next door — heavier popular cinema, not interchangeable with a quiet mystery.

October 1 for historical mystery

October 1 is a murder investigation in a town about to become independent. Political air, a detective, a clock. Choose it when you want a measured mystery rather than a street shootout. Kunle Afolayan; the catalogue page is the place for details.

The Figurine for supernatural unease

The Figurine is Afolayan in a more supernatural key. Belief is part of the tension: what the object might do, and what fear does to the people around it. Use it if folklore is the itch, not if you wanted a clean police procedural.

The Black Book, Brotherhood, Gangs of Lagos

The Black Book is a father pushing into a corrupt system. Brotherhood puts family loyalty under crime pressure. Gangs of Lagos is the contemporary street-crime film. These three are the direct, present-day pressure group. They are not the same movie. Black Book is the hunt; Brotherhood is the bind; Gangs of Lagos is the city as a machine.

Where to go next

93 Days is not a thriller in the crime sense — it is the 2014 Lagos Ebola story, procedural fear. Mentioned so a "Nigerian tension" list does not pretend every tense film is a gangster picture. Start with October 1 if you want history-as-mystery. Figurine if you want unease. Black Book or Gangs of Lagos if you want now. The title pages carry trailers where BRYME has them; this guide will not invent streams.
