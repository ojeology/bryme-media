The Alien Franchise in Order: Which Films to Watch and Why

The Alien franchise in order: release years for the films on BRYME, a simple watch path, and how the series moves between horror and action.

Published 2026-08-17By BRYME Editorial Desk · Editorial guide · Last updated 2026-08-21 · Reading time: about 3 minutes

Alien movies in release order

There is no single studio-mandated Alien watch order. Release order is the default. These are the Alien-related titles BRYME actually lists, with catalogue years: Alien (1979). Aliens (1986). Alien 3 (1992). Alien: Resurrection (1997). Prometheus (2012). Alien: Romulus (2024). Predator (1987) and Predator 2 (1990) sit next door in the wider creature-hunt lane. They are not numbered Alien films. Alien: Covenant is not on BRYME yet, so it is not invented here.

Story order versus release order

Story order puts Prometheus before the original Alien, because it is a prequel about the engineers and the mess that leads toward the xenomorph. Release order keeps 1979 first, which is how most people met the series. If you have never seen any of them, start with Alien (1979) in release order. If you already know the original and want the prequel thread, watch Prometheus after Alien and Aliens, not instead of them. This is a map, not a spoiler sheet.

Alien (1979) and Aliens (1986)

Alien is a haunted-house film on a spaceship: one creature, a tightening hull, no marine squad. It is the best first film because everything after it extends or argues with it. It is also the purest horror entry on this list. Aliens is the turn into action — more creatures, soldiers, volume. If the first film felt too slow, this is the counterweight. Together they are the two films most people mean by "the Alien films."

Alien 3 and Alien: Resurrection

Alien 3 goes smaller and grimmer. Alien: Resurrection is stranger and more stylised. Both split opinion. Watch them if you want the original Ripley arc completed. Skip them on a first pass if you only wanted the 1979/1986 pairing. BRYME will not pretend they are secret masterpieces or secret disasters.

Prometheus and Alien: Romulus

Prometheus is Ridley Scott back on the myth: creators, wrecked missions, a different kind of body horror. It is slower and talkier than Aliens. Useful if you care how the universe was built. Not a replacement for the original. Alien: Romulus (2024) is the recent return that leans toward the first film's dread with later-series callbacks. A fair modern entry if you want one current Alien film without sitting the 1990s pair first. It still makes more sense after 1979.

Predator, on the side

Predator is a hunt-in-the-jungle film, not an Alien sequel. Watch it if the creature-as-hunter idea is the itch. Predator 2 continues that line in a city. Neither is required for the Alien numbered films.

A suggested order

Core path: Alien, then Aliens, then Alien: Romulus. Full original arc after that: Alien 3 and Alien: Resurrection. Prequel curiosity: Prometheus after you already know 1979. Begin with the original. Every other film on this page is easier once you have seen what started it.
