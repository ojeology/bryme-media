Movies Like Parasite: Class, Tension and Dark Surprises

Looking for movies like Parasite? These films share the class tension, the con, or the sudden darkness — and they stand on their own.

Published 2026-08-17By BRYME Editorial Desk · Editorial guide · Last updated 2026-08-21 · Reading time: about 3 minutes

What people mean by movies like Parasite

Looking for movies like Parasite usually means you want one of three things: a clever con inside a rich house, a film that turns from comedy to violence without warning, or a class story that does not lecture. Parasite does all three. Almost nothing else does. The useful next watch is the film that nails the piece you actually cared about. This is a watch-next list from titles already on BRYME. It is not a ranking of Korean cinema and not a claim that any of these films is a secret remake.

Best movies like Parasite to watch next

Memories of Murder is Bong Joon-ho before the big house. A small-town murder investigation that keeps missing the point, then the cost of that miss. If you liked Parasite because of Bong — the weather, the faces, the joke that curdles — start here. The Host is Bong again, with a monster and a family that will not stay organised. It is broader and funnier than Parasite. The class bite is still there: who gets help, who gets the river. The Handmaiden is Park Chan-wook: a con, a mansion, and a plan that keeps swapping who is using whom. If the scheme in Parasite was the hook, this is the denser, more ornate version. Oldboy is the other Park film people reach for. It is meaner and more enclosed. Use it if the late-film violence in Parasite is what stayed with you, not if you wanted the family satire. Burning is a slow class thriller: one man, one woman, one richer man who may be lying. The tension is social and sexual. Very little of Parasite's slapstick. A lot of its unease. The Housemaid puts a worker inside an employer's house and lets the rooms do the damage. If the architecture of Parasite — stairs, basement, smell — was the point, this is the closer cousin. Shoplifters is Japanese, not Korean. A found family surviving by small thefts. No basement twist. If the poverty-as-teamwork in Parasite moved you more than the blood, this is the humane fork. Mickey 17 is Bong in 2025, science fiction, clones, a nasty employer. It is not a street-level Seoul film. It is the same director asking who is disposable. Mentioned here because the name on the poster is the honest link, not because it repeats Parasite's plot.

Other Korean thrillers on BRYME

If you want the same country more than the same structure, BRYME also has Decision to Leave, The Chaser, I Saw the Devil and The Wailing. Those are crime, pursuit, and folk horror — not class comedies. They belong on a Korean-thriller shelf, not on a fake "exactly like Parasite" dump. For a wider mood map, use A Korean Cinema Starter Guide for Different Moods.

How to pick the next film

If the con was the pleasure, go to The Handmaiden. If Bong's tone was the pleasure, Memories of Murder or The Host. If the last-act shock was the pleasure, Oldboy — knowing it is a different kind of cruelty. If the family-and-money ache was the pleasure, Shoplifters. Almost no film matches all three of Parasite's tricks at once. That is the point of this list. For the film itself, the catalogue page is Parasite.
