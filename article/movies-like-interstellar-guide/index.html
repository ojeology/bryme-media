Movies Like Interstellar: Six Films If the Cosmos Wasn't Enough

Looking for movies like Interstellar? These six films share the space travel, the time ache, or the family line — without copying Nolan's plot.

Published 2026-08-12By BRYME Editorial Desk · Editorial guide · Last updated 2026-08-21 · Reading time: about 2 minutes

What people mean by movies like Interstellar

Looking for movies like Interstellar usually means you want one of three things: the voyage, the time puzzle, or the father-daughter wait. Interstellar does all three. The useful next watch is the film that nails the piece you actually sat with. This is a spoiler-light watch-next list from titles already on BRYME. It is not a ranking of science fiction and not a recap of the ending — that sits in Interstellar Ending Explained: What Cooper’s Journey Means, after you have watched.

Best movies like Interstellar to watch next

Arrival is the quiet one. Aliens, language, a mother, time that does not run the way you expect. If the big idea in Interstellar mattered more than the docking, start here. The Martian is the problem-solving fork: one person, a planet, jokes that do not cancel the danger. Choose it when you wanted the science as work, not as a sermon. Gravity is shorter and meaner. Orbit, debris, very little philosophy. Use it for the physical fear of space, not for Cooper and Murph. Dune: Part Two and Blade Runner 2049 are the scale-and-design pair, both Denis Villeneuve. Sand and rain, not a wormhole. If IMAX and politics were the hook, these are closer than another space-dad film. 2001: A Space Odyssey is the older monument. Slow, cold, and the film Interstellar keeps arguing with. Not a casual Friday. Honest if you want the ancestor, not the sequel.

If you wanted Nolan more than space

If the director was the point, Christopher Nolan Movies in Order: Where to Start and What to Watch Next is the map of the films BRYME actually has — including Oppenheimer and The Odyssey. Do not start that path with Tenet.

How to pick

Idea and feeling: Arrival. Survival and humour: The Martian. Panic in orbit: Gravity. World and images: Dune: Part Two or Blade Runner 2049. The old argument: 2001. The catalogue page for the film itself is Interstellar.
